Proposed highlights

1. Methodology for analyzing proton multiplicity fluctuations with azimuthal partitions in heavy-ion collisionsPhys. Rev. C 110, 064905 (2024) · first author, 5 authors · arXiv:2406.00072

Introduces the azimuthal-partition observable and its statistical treatment, with simulation studies of its response to effects such as proton clustering.

  • Peer-reviewed, first-author paper presenting an observable I developed.
  • Directly connected to the thesis, so the two together tell a single story.
  • A methods paper; the physics results themselves are in the thesis.
2. PhD thesis: Exploring the QCD Phase Diagram at STARUCLA, 2023 · sole author · PDF

Measurement of proton multiplicity fluctuations in azimuthal partitions in Au+Au collisions from the RHIC Beam Energy Scan, as a probe of a possible first-order phase transition in the QCD phase diagram. Awarded the BNL RHIC & AGS Users' Meeting Thesis Award (2024).

  • Entirely my own work; the most complete account of the analysis.
  • Recognised by a thesis award.
  • Not a journal publication, and a thesis is less readily cited.

Options for the third publication

A. Measurement of isolated prompt photon production in p+p collisions at √s = 200 GeV with the sPHENIX detectorsPHENIX Collaboration · 2026 preprint · arXiv:2607.03892

An sPHENIX measurement of the isolated prompt photon cross section in p+p collisions, compared with PYTHIA and pQCD calculations.

My involvement: I contributed the cross-section measurement that the paper's luminosity rests on. The integrated luminosity (64.4 pb-1) is determined from the minimum-bias trigger cross section measured in a Vernier scan, which I measured (internal analysis note sPH-VernierScan-2024-001), and its uncertainty enters the systematics. I was not involved in the rest of the analysis, and I am an author as an sPHENIX collaboration member.

  • A concrete, identifiable contribution of mine: an absolute cross-section measurement that the paper's normalisation depends on.
  • Recent and current: it comes from my postdoctoral work with sPHENIX.
  • Shows detector and calibration expertise, which is closer to my present experimental work than the flow and fluctuation analyses.
  • My contribution is one input (the luminosity), not the photon analysis itself.
  • A 2026 preprint, so no citations yet; large collaboration.
  • The physics (prompt photons) is further from the thesis and the methods paper.
B. Precision Measurement of Net-Proton-Number Fluctuations in Au+Au Collisions at RHICSTAR Collaboration · Phys. Rev. Lett. 135, 142301 (2025) · arXiv:2504.00817

A STAR measurement of net-proton-number fluctuations across the Beam Energy Scan, bearing on the search for a QCD critical point.

My involvement: I was not directly involved in this analysis. I was a member of the working group (Correlations and Fluctuations) in which it was carried out and helped with some cross-checks, and I am an author as a STAR collaboration member.

  • The best-cited item on my list (roughly 118 citations at the time of writing), in a high-impact journal.
  • Same physics programme as the thesis and the methods paper, so it extends the same story.
  • Collaboration paper with several hundred authors, and I did not carry out the analysis, so it does not demonstrate my own contribution.
  • My role (cross-checks) is less specific and harder to point to than in option A.
