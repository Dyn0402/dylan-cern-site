Proposed highlights

1. PhD thesis: Exploring the QCD Phase Diagram at STARUCLA, 2023 · sole author · PDF

Measurement of proton multiplicity fluctuations in azimuthal partitions in Au+Au collisions from the RHIC Beam Energy Scan, as a probe of a possible first-order phase transition in the QCD phase diagram. Awarded the BNL RHIC & AGS Users' Meeting Thesis Award (2024).

  • Entirely my own work; the most complete account of the analysis.
  • Recognised by a thesis award.
  • Not a journal publication, and a thesis is less readily cited.
2. Methodology for analyzing proton multiplicity fluctuations with azimuthal partitions in heavy-ion collisionsPhys. Rev. C 110, 064905 (2024) · first author, 5 authors · arXiv:2406.00072

Introduces the azimuthal-partition observable and its statistical treatment, with simulation studies of its response to effects such as proton clustering.

  • Peer-reviewed, first-author paper presenting an observable I developed.
  • Directly connected to the thesis, so the two together tell a single story.
  • A methods paper; the physics results themselves are in the thesis.

Options for the third publication

A. Precision Measurement of Net-Proton-Number Fluctuations in Au+Au Collisions at RHICSTAR Collaboration · Phys. Rev. Lett. 135, 142301 (2025) · arXiv:2504.00817

A STAR measurement of net-proton-number fluctuations across the Beam Energy Scan, bearing on the search for a QCD critical point.

My involvement: I was not directly involved in this analysis. I was a member of the working group (Correlations and Fluctuations) in which it was carried out and gave occasional feedback, and I am an author as a STAR collaboration member.

  • The best-cited item on my list (roughly 118 citations at the time of writing), in a high-impact journal.
  • Same physics programme as the thesis and the methods paper.
  • Collaboration paper with several hundred authors, and I did not carry out the analysis, so it does not demonstrate my own contribution.
