LNL, Legnaro: the ATOMKI ⁸Be search with MX17

INFN LNL · AN2000 / CN · ⁷Li(p,e⁺e⁻)⁸Be · started 7 October 2026 · Geant4 8 October 2026

Repeat the original ATOMKI measurement — a ~1 MeV proton captured on ⁷Li, the 18.15 MeV state of ⁸Be decaying by internal pair creation — with the four Micromegas arms instead of plastic telescopes. Rates and yields are analytic, anchored to data; the detector is a full Geant4 simulation of the apparatus around a lithium target, with a selection that uses no Monte Carlo truth. Target proposed on 11 Nov: a flat aluminium end cap at 45° with LNL’s pressed lithium.

The facility

  • AN20000.2–2 MV belt Van de Graaff (1971); H⁺ ≤ 1 µA on the angled lines, 0° line ≤ 20 nA; self-service, one energy change a day
  • CN0.8–5.5 MV; 1 µA in the beam sheet at 0.8–2 MeV, ~4 µA authorised; pulsed 3 MHz, < 2 ns, ≤ 700 nA; 7 lines, one hall; Mon–Fri daytime
  • AccessLNL PAC, yearly in autumn (2025: deadline 3 Sep, meeting 8–10 Oct); contacts A. Selva, pacbeams@lnl.infn.it
  • Already done hereLNL's own ⁸Be pair spectrometer (Marchi, Góngora-Servín et al.): AN2000, 2023–24, LiF, ~800 nA, ~790 h — unpublished

The reaction

Capture makes ⁸Be at E* = 17.255 + ⅞E_p MeV. Two M1 resonances do the selecting — 441 keV → 17.64 MeV (σ 5.9 mb) and 1030 keV → 18.15 MeV — but a flat E1 direct capture runs underneath, and at 1.03–1.10 MeV it is half of the ground-state γ. E1 pairs are flat in angle, so they fill the 140° region. X17 pairs pile up above an edge at 134–139°.

The target: where it stands (11 Nov 2026)

On 11 Nov every target design was checked against how lithium targets are really made, at LNL and elsewhere (lnl/target/TARGET_REVIEW.md). This is a proposal. It has not been discussed with Pierfrancesco yet.

  • Proposed: a one-piece aluminium end cap with a flat face at 45°. Al 6061-T6, face 0.4–0.5 mm over a Ø20 bore, a Cu layer (≥ 12 µm along the beam) that stops the beam, and LNL's own rolled lithium pressed on with flat moulds. Flat parts only, and the film is uniform along the beam by geometry. Days: 0.31 d with big plastics, 4.2 / 6.5 d as built (estimate from the 45° diamond window’s Geant4, Geant4 for the cap is running (run L14)).
  • If the ogive is kept, the order is: first Pierfrancesco's Cu ogive, electroformed in hard copper, with the Li pressed on the tip only. Then the Al ogive (0.4 mm) with Cu plated over the whole inside. The 4 Nov design (Cu patch, masked evaporated Li) is possible but a development project of months, and as built it gains only ×1.0–1.7 on the Cu ogive.
  • Lithium 10–12 µm, not 20. Residual water turns lithium into LiOH, which slows the beam more. With half of a 20 µm film converted, 52 % of the γ₀ comes from the 441 keV resonance at 1.10 MeV. A 12 µm film stays at 10 % even fully converted, for 51–61 % of the yield (10–12 µm). How fast the film converts at LNL's vacuum is not known. A one-week film test decides.
  • Dropped: every beryllium part (no supplier of machined Be vacuum parts since 2023), every molybdenum stop at 1 µA (its K x-rays fill the Micromegas) and the diamond (at 1.1 W an aluminium face is enough). The thin LiF / Li₂O film of the October study is not a fallback at LNL: such films lasted hours to two days on AN2000.
  • Nothing is deposited inside the beam pipe. No laboratory does that. LNL rolls and presses lithium in an argon glovebox.

The Geant4 reference study (8–12 Oct) used the ATOMKI-style baseline: a few-hundred-nm film of Li₂O on a 10 µm Al foil in a thin carbon-fibre chamber, thin enough that the beam never slows into the 50× stronger 441 keV resonance. That target is no longer the plan (above), but the physics of the study carries over: the film, not the shell, sets the line mix.

Rates and reach (Geant4, 8 Oct 2026)

At ATOMKI's conditions (Li₂O 300 µg/cm², 1.10 MeV, 1 µA): ~1.9×10³ γ₀/s and ~7 IPC pairs/s into 4π, ~900 X17/day at the ATOMKI ratio R = 5.8×10⁻⁶. Both leptons reach the Micromegas in 39 % of X17 decays, but the as-built trigger (two 20×30×2 cm plastics per arm) keeps only 3.3 %; four 75×75×5 cm slabs keep 23 %.

The energy sum separates the 15 MeV (γ₁) transitions from the 18 MeV ones — as built only with the liquid scintillators read out, cleanly with 5 cm plastics.

Cosmics are the one background that can sink it: ~8×10⁴ two-arm pairs/day in 125–155° at trigger level, against ~110 IPC. An upper E_sum edge (muons leave 20–35 MeV), then 3° Micromegas segments or a ~0.3 ns time-of-flight veto, bring them to ~1/day. The measured chambers' 15° segments are not enough. EPC, accidentals and DAQ dead time are negligible.

Is the significance real? (12 Oct 2026)

In the 130–160° window the X17 is only ~7 % of the background, so it cannot be seen by eye, and the table above assumes the IPC angular shape is known exactly. Once the fit may also bend the shape, the numbers change: the acceptance can be a free cubic, and there can be 2 % of unknown structure on the scale of the peak. With big plastics, 1.2 d becomes 26 d; as built, years.

The fix is ATOMKI's own: symmetric pairs. An X17 at its edge angle splits its energy evenly between the leptons; the IPC at 140° is often lopsided. |y| = |E₁ − E₂|/(E₁ + E₂) < 0.3, from the two arm energies, keeps 69 % of the X17 and 42 % of the background: S/B 7 → 18 %. The asymmetric pairs and the 15 MeV side band of the same run then measure the detector's shape.

Better still: fit the pair mass. The total energy W = 18.15 MeV is known, so the opening angle and the energy split give m_ee = W √((1 − y²)(1 − cos θ)/2). The X17 becomes a 0.6 MeV-wide peak, and a few % of shape error cannot fake it. With 2 % unknown structure, the in-run control and ideal Geant4 energies: ~0.8 d with big plastics, ~8 d as built, on ×10–20 more IPC Monte Carlo and including the IPC's anisotropy about the beam. As built, the IPC has a geometric hump near 17 MeV under the signal, which the Monte Carlo has to get right.

Fit in three variables (12 Oct 2026). With an energy response anchored on what n_TOF measured, the ideal-energy pair-mass numbers above become 1.7 d (big plastics) and ~40 d (as built). The y resolution smears m_ee. A fit that keeps the angle, the energy split and the energy sum as separate axes, θ × |y| × E_sum, keeps that information: ~1.0 d with big plastics, ~17 d as built (liquid repaired), with the same unknown background structure imposed on every fit. It is also the robust one. At fixed E_sum the X17 is a curve in (θ, |y|); an acceptance error is a vertical band, a γ₁ leak a band in E_sum, an m_ee error a diagonal, and the signal-free parts of the plane measure each. No new data are needed: θ, E₁ and E₂ are in every pair event.

ATOMKI's bump is not much bigger. In PRL 116 042501 Fig. 4 the data sit +25–35 % above their simulated IPC curve: acceptance-corrected, not background-subtracted, on a log scale. Their 6.8σ assumes that curve's shape too. Their chamber scatters as ours does; the |y| cut and a smaller E1 share make their S/B ~3× ours. In our generator, their figure needs R ≈ 1.2×10⁻⁵, so our signal assumption is the conservative end.
