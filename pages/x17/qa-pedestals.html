DREAM pedestal stability

56 pedestal acquisitions · 8 FEUs · 4 096 channels · 01 Jul – 10 Aug 2026

Before every run the DAQ measured what each of the 4 096 channels does when nothing is happening, and kept the result. Six weeks of those measurements say how steady the electronics were, and they are specific enough to date a cable coming loose to the afternoon. Every number here is re-derived from the decoded pedestal runs on EOS rather than read out of the firmware's own summary — though the two are compared, and they agree.

Stable to a few per cent for weeks at a time, and then three things moved.

One was ours and global. On 23 July around 13:00 the DREAM readout clock divider went from 6 to 4, and the per-channel residual noise rose by ×2.0 on all eight FEUs at once and stayed there for the last 18 days — the whole production period. The coherent common mode did not move (×1.07), so this is a real degradation of the incoherent noise floor, not a rescaling.

Two were chamber A's and physical. From 21 July its common mode went up ×3.3 on all sixteen chips of both views, and from 22 July its x-view connector 8 — 64 strips — was electrically disconnected. Both were fixed at one access on 27 July between 11:23 and 14:11. The dead connector covers every sub-run of run_79, the first long production run, and the hits confirm it: 41 of those 64 channels recorded nothing at all.

Everything else is quiet. Baselines held, no chamber drifted, and the other connector dropouts recovered within days.

What a pedestal here measures

Each acquisition is a no-trigger readout of all eight FEUs — 512 channels × 32 samples × about a thousand events each. Every channel is decomposed the same way, and the three numbers answer different questions.

  • Raw σ The spread of the raw ADC about the channel's own baseline. On these detectors it is dominated by coherent pickup rather than by the amplifier — at the end of the run the raw σ is about eight times the residual.
  • Common mode Per chip, per time sample, the median over its 64 channels of (amplitude − channel mean). Its RMS is the coherent swing that chip sees. Same definition as the end-of-run report, so the numbers on the two pages are the same numbers.
  • Residual σ What is left of a channel once that common mode is subtracted. This sets the 5 σ zero-suppression threshold, so it is what sensitivity actually rests on.

Two checks that the decomposition is honest. The raw spread is not a fixed waveform shape repeated every event — the event-averaged shape carries under 0.1 % of the raw variance, so raw σ really is noise. And the residual agrees with the firmware's own per-channel σ, the one it used to program the thresholds and which it derived from a separate acquisition, at a median r = 0.98 with 81 % of FEU-acquisitions above 0.9. Ratios and steps are the trustworthy part of this page; the last digit of any single sigma is not.

23 July: the readout clock, and a doubled noise floor

Between the pedestal at 10:05 on 23 July and the one at 16:47, the residual noise rose on all eight FEUs — by ×1.74 to ×2.46. Over the same boundary the common mode moved by at most ×1.25 and the baseline by under 20 ADC. A gain change would have scaled all three together; only the incoherent part moved, so the per-channel noise floor genuinely got worse.

residual beforeafterstep common mode beforeafterstep

Exactly one configuration parameter changed at that boundary. The DREAM readout clock divider RdClk_Div went 6 → 4 while the sampling clock WrClk_Div stayed at 6 — the ADC digitises 1.5× faster into the same 60 ns sampling period. Everything else the FEUs were programmed with is byte-identical across the step, including Feu_Pwr_Dream, the DREAM registers, the polarity masks, latency and the 32-sample window. The HV was identical too: 200 V on all eight channels, powered, for every pedestal quoted on this page.

The campaign ran three clock configurations, and it is the combination rather than the readout speed alone that matters: the first epoch also used RdClk_Div 4 and was quiet, but with WrClk_Div 2 beside it. That is an observation, not a mechanism — a pedestal cannot say why.

Chamber A, 21–27 July

On 21 July at 12:04 chamber A's common mode went from 31 to 103 ADC on the x view and 23 to 77 on the y — a factor 3.3, on all sixteen chips of both FEUs at once and on no other chamber. A fault on one cable moves one chip; sixteen chips moving together is a shared ground, shield or supply path at the chamber. The next pedestal, on 22 July at 10:13, shows x-view connector 8 gone as well — its raw noise down from about 32 ADC to under 5, which is what a preamp with nothing on its input reads.

Both were fixed at the same intervention. The pedestal at 27 July 11:23 still has them; the one at 14:11 has neither, and A is back to 34 / 25 ADC — its pre-21-July values to within a few per cent.

This lands on run_79. The window covers runs 67 to 82, all 16 sub-runs of run_79 among them — the first long statistics run at the production setpoint. Read straight off the hits of run_79/stat090_0001, connector 8 of A-x delivered 3.7 % of that FEU's hits against 11.3 % in run_100 after the fix, 41 of its 64 channels recorded no hits at all, and the hits that did survive sit in a narrow band at threshold (median amplitude 30, 90th percentile 34) rather than the wide spectrum of a live connector (median 192, 90th percentile 3 467). The pedestal diagnosis and the physics data agree.

Where the fleet finished

State at the last pedestal of the campaign, 10 August 09:12. Baseline, spread, σ and threshold in ADC counts; "coherent" is raw σ divided by residual σ; the last two columns are channels out of 512. A channel counts as disconnected when its raw σ is below 12 % of its FEU's median or below 8 ADC outright, and loud when its residual is more than 3× the FEU median — both relative to the same acquisition, so the 23 July scale change does not masquerade as four thousand new dead channels.

What this does not establish

  • Not that the 23 July change hurt the physics A pedestal measures noise, never signal. If the clock change also raised the signal amplitude, the signal-to-noise ratio could be unchanged or better. Settling it needs hit amplitudes across the boundary, which is a different measurement; what is established here is that the noise floor doubled and the threshold with it.
  • Not which physical object moved A pedestal measures a FEU and its cable, not a chamber. "Chamber A's common mode tripled" means the electronics chain labelled A did; separating chamber, cabling and FEU needs the record of which board sat where.
  • Not a continuous history These are 56 snapshots over six weeks, some hours apart and some days. Anything that started and finished between two of them leaves no trace here at all.
  • Not the state before 1 July The first pedestal preserved in the campaign area is 1 July 19:52. Data taking began on 28 June, and the 30 June commissioning set lives outside this tree.
  • Not a claim about the other dropouts' effect on data Only the run_79 case was checked against hits. The early-July ones fall in the commissioning period and the 9 August one lasted at most three hours, but neither was confirmed in the recorded data the way run_79 was.

Provenance

Built by ntof_pedestal_qa/ in the analysis repository, in three read-only passes over /eos/experiment/ntof/data/x17/july_beam/ and then everything else locally. lxplus/extract_pedestals.py decodes every pedestal ROOT into per-channel mean, raw σ, residual σ and per-chip common mode, and parses the firmware's own _ped.aux / _thr.aux alongside for comparison. lxplus/extract_usage.py walks all 2 695 sub-runs for their pedestal_run.txt, which is what makes "which pedestal was in force" a fact rather than an inference. lxplus/extract_context.py records the configuration and HV each pedestal was taken under, which is what makes a clock change distinguishable from a cable change. export_site.py freezes the result into data/x17-pedestals.json, which is what this page reads; the same analysis also writes a longer report with static figures, published as a note — that one goes channel by channel through chamber A, the baselines, the channel-health counts and how stale each pedestal got, and it reads offline.

The chamber↔FEU map is ntof_active_area/clusters.py: A = FEU 3/4, B = 5/6, C = 7/8, D = 1/2. The run-level QA that sits beside this one is the DREAM run table, and the campaign as a whole is in the end-of-run report.
