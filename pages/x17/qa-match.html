Two independent DAQs recorded the same beam on two independent clocks, and neither is physics on its own: the Micromegas measure the pair, the n_TOF scintillators say when the neutron arrived. This page is the join between them — how each piece of beam was put on one clock, how well it worked, and how much of it is not joined yet.

Every pulse since run_79, and what became of it

The unit that matters is the pulse: one proton pulse on the target, one DREAM burst of triggers. Since run_79, when the trigger was locked to the wall+plastic coincidence, every burst of every sub-run has exactly one terminal state in the pulse ledger — matched, or a named reason why not. A pulse counts as matched only when it is joined to an n_TOF bunch and at least 80 % of its triggers have a wall+plastic partner inside ±25 ns; a wrong join measures 0.00 %, so there is no grey zone. Pulses with no beam behind them (empty pulses, cosmic-bounce blocks, trigger modes without the coincidence) are shown but are not ours to match and stay out of the denominator.

What a segment is, and why the clock is fitted per bunch

A segment is one DREAM sub-run crossed with the one n_TOF run that was taking beam at the same moment. It is the smallest thing that can carry its own clock fit, and the reason is physical: the DREAM timestamp clock wanders about a part per million from burst to burst, which is 60 ns of drift across an 80 ms bunch — several times the width of the accept window. A single offset and rate for a whole run cannot absorb that, so the fit carries a per-bunch offset and rate correction on top:

t_nTOF = t_DREAM · (1 + K + δkb) + T₀ + aarm + δab
accept  |t_candidate − t_nTOF| < 25 ns

K and T₀ are the clock rate ratio and offset, a_arm is a fixed offset per detector arm, and the two δ terms are fitted per bunch. None of these transfer. The same constants fitted on a different processing of the same run leave a 45 ns offset and a 1.35 % rate error, so every segment is fitted from its own data and nothing reads a stored constant.

The clock is fully fitted before anything is cut. No hit is ever discarded on a provisional calibration: the fit runs on a candidate list, and only then is every scintillator hit within ±1 µs of the corrected prediction written out — along with the same width taken 100 µs later, which is the control window the accidental rate is measured from.

Is the per-bunch fit honest?

It is fitted on matched triggers and then used to match, which is a real circularity and was audited rather than argued away. About a hundred matched triggers per bunch constrain two parameters; a split-half test gives ρ = +0.996, which is 0.92 ppm of real drift against 0.06 ppm of fit noise; and the in-sample to cross-validated gap is a few tenths of a percent, reported per segment in the table above.

The decisive test is different: efficiency in a ±500 ns window is identical to five decimal places with and without the per-bunch correction. The correction concentrates matches into the accept window; it does not create them.

What the QA checks, and what it cannot see

Each joined segment is judged against nineteen absolute checks — the fitted rate in a physical range, the coarse peak above its floor, efficiency, accidental rate, cross-validation gap, residual centring and width, arm offsets against the reference run, per-bunch scatter, whether flash triggers correctly carry no hits, and whether the coincidence is contained in the slim window rather than cut by it. On top of that sits a population test: a segment can pass every absolute check and still be the only one in the campaign whose T₀ sits 300 ns from its neighbours, and that is the failure mode that produced a silently mis-timed slim in the first place. Population outliers are reported and never silently upgraded into a failure — being unusual is a reason to look, not proof of being wrong.

The joined segments pass their checks, and that number has to be read with the failures beside it. A mis-joined segment fails its clock fit and writes no file, so the QA layer never sees it. The QA layer is clean because the broken segments are absent. That is exactly why this page counts pulses rather than green rows, and why the segments that produced nothing are rows here rather than a footnote.

What went wrong, and how the join now decides

The physics was never the problem. At the right burst-to-pulse lock, about 96 % of a pulse’s triggers have a wall+plastic partner inside ±25 ns; at a wrong lock the same measurement gives 0.00 % — three orders of magnitude, per pulse, measured over ~190 candidate evaluations across the campaign, and the 20–80 % band was never entered. The trouble was that a weaker instrument was allowed to decide: pulse_match’s count scan compares cluster counts at 50 ms tolerance and is degenerate under the PS supercycle — locks 14.4 s apart tie to within a cluster or two — and it silently kept one, shifting every bunch number by tens of bunches. That is what failed 107 segments in the 12 August campaign and quietly mis-locked 14 more by a margin of two clusters.

Since 13 August the coincidence chooses. The count scan only enumerates candidate locks; every segment then runs the production join and clock fit on a sample of bunches per candidate and accepts the first whose median pulse reaches 80 % coincidence, or refuses with the measurements attached. That resolved 99 of the 112 unmatched segments and recovered the three guard-blocked ones — including run_139/0010, whose true lock was the exact candidate the count scan had vetoed by a margin of 25. Wrong locks accepted campaign-wide: none measured. Three remaining “dark hours” (run_116/0027, run_118/0017, run_122/0000) had every candidate inside the ±120 s enumeration measure 0 %; a ±200-bunch scan found their locks at +172.8 s — exactly four supercycles, outside the window — and they are being re-joined with the enumeration widened.

What is left is named pulse by pulse in the ledger above: pulses in gaps of the n_TOF DAQ (run transitions, one 25-minute reset), the last few sub-runs whose slim jobs died on disk and were never re-run, and a tail of pulses joined correctly but scoring 73–80 % against the 80 % bar — small bursts, usable as they are. Nothing in the residue points at the detectors or the data.

Provenance

Numbers on this page come from three places, joined by scripts/freeze_x17_match.py in this site’s repository: the per-segment clock_qa.json records the slim pipeline writes beside each output file (newest vintage per segment); the slim job summaries, which is what makes failed segments visible at all; and the pulse ledger (ntof_processing/slim_pipeline/pulse_ledger.py), which gives every DREAM burst since run_79 one terminal state from the burst census, the beam record, the n_TOF PKUP times and the per-pulse coincidence arrays. The segment universe is every (sub-run, n_TOF run) pair with beam overlap, from segments.py. The calibration itself is documented in ntof_dream_merge/DREAM_NTOF_CALIBRATION.md, and the machine- readable constants for the reference pair live in nTof_x17_DAQ/calibrations/dream_ntof/. Two working notes cover the ground in more depth: the clock QA note, which is where the apparent ±150 ns window was resolved into per-bunch drift, and the join mislock note.
