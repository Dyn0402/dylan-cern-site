Searching for X17 at n_TOF

2025 – present · Affiliated Researcher, The University of Manchester · CERN n_TOF

A series of nuclear-transition anomalies point at a possible ~17 MeV boson. We are testing that claim in a channel nobody has used before, with a pressurized ³He target and four micro-TPC tracking walls at CERN's neutron time-of-flight facility.

The anomaly

Beginning with 8Be, the ATOMKI group reported an excess of e+e− pairs at large opening angles in nuclear transitions, later echoed in 4He and 12C. The excess sits where ordinary internal pair creation is falling steeply, and its position is consistent across nuclei with the two-body decay of a ~17 MeV boson — the kinematic angle for a particle of mass m emitted with energy E is θ ≈ 2·asin(mc²/E). Independent confirmation in an unrelated system is the obvious next step, and that is what this experiment is for.

The measurement

We look at ³He(n, e⁺e⁻)⁴He at CERN's n_TOF facility. The neutron time-of-flight beam gives us the incident energy event by event, which is what makes a thermal-neutron-driven measurement tractable here.

  • Channel³He(n, e⁺e⁻)⁴He
  • Target³He at 500 atm
  • Tracking4 × micro-TPC Micromegas
  • Wall size40 × 40 × 3 cm
  • Calorimetrylayered scintillator, SiPM read
  • Trigger6 × CAEN N1081B

The target vessel itself matters more than it looks: a 20 mm diameter, 40 mm long aluminium capsule with a 0.6 mm wall and a 0.9 mm CFRP wrap. Its hemispherical end caps change the material budget seen along the beam direction substantially compared with a flat-ended cylinder, which feeds directly into both the vertex sampling and the conversion background.

My role

  • Technical lead for the DREAM-based DAQ — the software front end and the full acquisition chain, plus a new DAQ rack assembled at CEA Saclay from spare front-end boards.
  • On-site contact person for the 40-day data-taking run: commissioning, flammable-gas operations, and the scintillator trigger logic across six programmable logic units.
  • Operations infrastructure — fifteen standing monitor processes with automated alerting on beam loss and acquisition faults, a live shift dashboard with per-wall HV and run statistics, and automated EOS transfer with disk-space triage of every recorded run.
  • Calibrations — per-channel gamma-flash time constants referenced to the beam pickup, a DREAM-to-n_TOF time map that resolved an apparent ±150 ns window into per-bunch clock drift, and a pulser-based characterization of the readout window.
  • Geant4 studies establishing feasibility of the thermal-neutron measurement, which drove the redesign around SiPM-read plastic and liquid-scintillator calorimeter layers; extended to optimize the thermal-gate trigger and to decompose the aluminium (n, γ) background into double-Compton and pair-production contributions.

Reconstruction

Positions, angles and drift depths come from a forward-model fit to the waveforms, not from per-strip hit times. A hit time is an aggregate over resistively shared charge and compresses the drift ladder by 20–30 %, so hits are used for cluster finding, efficiency and QA only. Anything that produces a position, an angle or a depth goes through the waveform fit.
