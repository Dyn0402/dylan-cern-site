Micromegas & micro-pattern gaseous detectors

CEA Paris-Saclay · micro-TPC construction · µRWELL in SPS H4 · CyMBaL for ePIC

Most of this work happens one centimeter above an anode plane: building gaseous trackers, putting them in front of cosmics and test beams, and finding out where they stop behaving.

How a Micromegas works

A charged particle ionizes the gas in a few-millimeter drift gap. The electrons drift to a micromesh, and a thin ~100 µm gap below it at very high field multiplies each electron by ~104. The charge induced on segmented anode strips gives the position. Run it as a micro-TPC and the arrival-time ladder across strips additionally gives you the track angle and depth from a single plane.

Detectors I have built and characterized

Micro-TPC walls for the X17 search

Helped build the 40 × 40 × 3 cm micro-TPC Micromegas detectors now used in the n_TOF X17 experiment, and characterized them on a cosmic-ray bench at Saclay before installation.

µRWELL in the SPS H4 beamline

Measured efficiency and charge-spreading response, and identified a non-amplifying region that no operating voltage could recover — a manufacturing defect rather than an operating point, which is the kind of conclusion a test beam is uniquely able to settle.

CyMBaL — MPGDs for ePIC

Prototype testing for CyMBaL, the barrel tracking layer of the ePIC detector at the future Electron-Ion Collider. I retrofitted a cosmic muon telescope with a silicon tracker for rapid prototype QA, built a synchronized DAQ with configuration bookkeeping, and simulated multiple scattering to pick test-beam energies and configurations. Written up in PoS QNP2024, 011.

PICOSEC

A Cherenkov-based Micromegas pushing time resolution toward tens of picoseconds. My involvement here is exploratory rather than central.

Simulation

Gas properties come from Garfield++ transfer-table calculations (drift velocity, diffusion and Townsend coefficients for the He/C₂H₆ mixtures in use), run on the CERN batch system rather than locally.
