Quark–gluon plasma & the STAR Beam Energy Scan

2016 – 2023 · Ph.D., UCLA · advisor Huan Huang · STAR at RHIC

Microseconds after the Big Bang the universe was a quark–gluon plasma. Colliding heavy nuclei recreates droplets of it — and the open question is whether the transition back to ordinary matter passes through a critical point.

Why fluctuations

Near a critical point, correlations grow without bound, and conserved quantities should fluctuate more than independent emission allows. Protons carry baryon number and are measurable event by event, so the event-by-event proton multiplicity distribution across the Beam Energy Scan — which walks the collision energy, and therefore the baryon chemical potential, across the phase diagram — is a natural place to look for that excess.

Azimuthal partitions

The standard approach uses cumulants of the full-acceptance multiplicity distribution, which are statistically fragile and sensitive to volume fluctuations. I developed a novel observable using azimuthal partitions: divide each event into azimuthal wedges and compare the proton count distribution within them against the binomial baseline that independent emission would produce. The deviation is directly sensitive to proton clustering, which is what a first-order phase transition would produce.

The method paper is Phys. Rev. C 110, 064905 (2024), and the full treatment is in my Ph.D. thesis.
