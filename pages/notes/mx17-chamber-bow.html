Details

Verdict. The bow is real at the level of "the readout plane is not flat by 1–3 mm and the drift direction follows its slope"; it is seen by three routes that do not share their systematics, with the sign the geometry predicts, on every chamber. It is static per chamber: 19 runs (other subruns, other nights, both bench slots, other drift and mesh voltages) give each chamber the same depth within ~2σ, and the drift-tilt shape measured on the golden run transfers to the other runs with only the rigid tilt re-measured. Which load makes it (gas overpressure, mesh tension, weight) cannot be split with the data we have.

What it changes: the angle conversion (a position-dependent drift tilt, ±0.6 µm/ns across the chamber) and the position of the mesh plane (±tan·h, up to ~0.5 mm at tan 0.3). A correction measured from positions alone predicts most of the angle improvement on held-out tracks.

What a bowed readout board does to a micro-TPC, and four ways to see it

Side view, sag exaggerated ~20×. Throughout: hover points and cells for values, counts and errors.

2

Details

Frame. M3 z points up; the chambers lie readout-side down with the cathode on top (slide 8 pins both), so electrons drift down onto the mesh. h(x, y) is the height of the mesh surface above the flat plane the alignment assumes; a dish has h lowest at the centre.

P, positions. A track of slope tan = dx/dz crosses a mesh lowered by −h at a point shifted by tan·h. In every position cell the slope of the residual (fitted p0 − M3 prediction) against tan is h there. Uses only fitted positions and M3.

A, drift tilt. The fit measures w = dx/dt of the arriving charge. If the drift lines leave the mesh at right angles to it, charge from height d lands d·dh/dx away, so w gains w0 = v·r_f·dh/dx, with r_f = 1 if the cathode follows the bow (drift lines tilted all the way up) and r_f = ½ if the cathode stays flat (the tilt falls linearly from the mesh to the cathode). For a dish, dh/dx changes sign across the chamber: w0 ramps from negative to positive.

W, narrowest cluster. The seed cluster (strips above threshold) is narrowest for the track that runs along the drift lines, at tan = −w0/v, not at tan = 0. This needs no fitted angle and no hit times, only which strips fired.

T, column duration. If the cathode stays flat, the gap is deeper at the centre by the dish depth and the column of charge takes longer to arrive. The ladder slope (w against M3 tan, a pure velocity measurement) separates a deeper gap from a slower gas.

Details

Each view measures the mesh height with its own strips and its own M3 projection (x residual against M3 tan_x, y residual against tan_y), so the two rows of maps share neither the readout layer nor the telescope coordinate. A real surface must look the same in both, including along the axis a view does not measure (the X strips see the dish along y through where the tracks cross, not through their own coordinate).

Quantitatively (2-D quadratic fit, event level): the curvature along x from the X view vs from the Y view, and along y likewise, agree with |pull| ≤ 3.6 (median 1.7). The one systematic pattern: the curvature along a view's own axis reads 20–40 % larger than the same curvature seen by the other view (kww from Y is larger than kww from X on all five chambers). Edge truncation acts along the own axis only, so the other-axis numbers are the conservative ones; they are still 1.0–2.2 mm per (100 mm)².

Details

Left. 20 numbers: for each chamber, the curvature along x and along y, measured once from the X strips and once from the Y strips. They scatter about the diagonal; the own-axis excess (slide 4 Details) is the visible pattern.

Right: could a t0 error fake a height? The fit's t0 and p0 slide together along the track: a t0 error δt moves p0 by w·δt = v·tan·δt, which reads as a height v·δt. If δt depended on position (per-chip timing, a column-shape effect), that would fake h(x, y). Moving every p0 along its own track to the median t0 of its trigger-time class (ftst) removes any such slide. The dish survives: the pinned curvature is 0.79–0.97 × the fitted one (chamber means), with more noise (t0 jitter of 50–100 ns adds 2–4 mm × tan of position noise).

Details

The band is computed from slide 3's parabola alone: w0_pred(p) = b·r_f·dh/dp, with b the local ladder slope (the drift velocity seen by the fit) and r_f between ½ (cathode flat) and 1 (cathode bowed with the board). Nothing in the band is fitted to the angles. The sign follows from the frame (z up, cathode up): a dish (h'' > 0) must make w0 rise with position. It does, on all ten views. That sign is predicted, not chosen.

What an M3 artefact cannot do: M3 errors that come from bowed telescope planes shift a track by tan·g and vanish for vertical tracks, so they cannot make w0 (defined at tan = 0) ramp. A differential plane-scale error between the M3 pairs could, but it would need ~14 % of strip-pitch error and would show as a 6 % position scale at the DUT (slide 9).

Details

A track parallel to the local drift lines puts all its charge on the fewest strips. If the drift lines are vertical that is the vertical track (tan = 0); if they lean by α, it is the track at tan = −α. Which way: the mesh centre is lower, away from the cathode, from the telescope axis and how the chambers sit

Each sign is tied to a geometry record or a measurement against the telescope, not to the bow fit

8

Details

The chain. (1) M3 z points up: the ray files carry Z_Up = 1302 mm and Z_Down = 24 mm, the heights of the top and bottom M3 planes, and the upper DUT slot (P2, z = 702) sits above the lower (P1, z = 232); bench levels in mpgd26/geometry.py. (2) The chambers lie cathode-up: each rests on a rail board with its mesh 5 mm above the board top, and, as a measurement, the fitted micro-TPC slope w has the same sign as the M3 slope on every chamber (kw > 0 in every bundle), which for tan = dx/dz_up means the drift depth grows upward. (3) h = d(residual)/d(tan) with tan = dx/dz_up is a height along z_up: h lowest at the centre = the mesh centre sits below its rim, further from the cathode. (4) Drift lines normal to such a mesh lean outward-down, so w0 must rise with position: observed on 10/10 views (slide 6). That last step is a prediction checked by the data, not an input.

All three candidate loads push the centre the same way on the bench: the board's weight (lying horizontal, readout side down), gas overpressure (pushes the readout board outward = down) and residual mesh tension (contracts the mesh face, curling the board concave towards the mesh: centre down relative to the clamped rim).

Details

M3 plane scale. A tan error proportional to position (what a linear w0 ramp would need from the telescope) requires M3 to mis-scale positions differently at its upper and lower plane pairs. With 1150 mm between the pairs and a ramp of ~1×10⁻⁴ per mm, that is ~14 % of differential pitch, impossible for etched strips, and it would leave a ~6 % position scale at a DUT between the pairs. The residual scale terms bu, bw are ≤ 0.13 mm per 100 mm on every chamber.

M3 planes bowed. If all four M3 planes sagged by the same g(x, y), every reconstructed track would be displaced by tan·g at every z, with its slope unchanged: the position route would read h_DUT − g. This cannot make the angle (w0) or width routes move, so those two prove the chamber's own drift lines tilt. But a common g could change the size of the position-route dish; the ratio r_f (slide 6) would then absorb it. Within r_f between ½ and 1, the chamber's own dish is between ~0.5× and ~1.3× the position number.

Details

Why T × b. The column duration T is gap / v. A v(x, y) map (gas, field) and a gap map both move T. The ladder slope b (fitted w against M3 tan) is v alone: the micro-TPC slope does not depend on how deep the gap is. So T × b is the gap with v(x, y) divided out. This is how the gap is separated from FA's v(x, y): the gap uses the ladder only as a local v and needs no v model.

What it shows. On det2 and det3 the X column reads longer in the middle, as a dish over a flat cathode predicts (1.5–2 mm), and the 2026-07-30 gap study saw the same thing (det2 "flat with a ~2 mm rim droop", det3 "deepest at the centre"). But X and Y, which time one and the same column, disagree: the fitted gap curvatures of the two views differ by 1–4 mm per (100 mm)², more than the 1–2 predicted. The column end depends on amplitude, on the Y-side resistive tail and on the 60 ns depth grid, all of which vary across a chamber. With q_uend or (q_u90 − q_u50), with or without charge matching, the picture is the same. So the column route neither confirms nor excludes the bow; it is listed as a limit, not as evidence.

Code: rerun/model_audit/followups/fb_bow_maps/gap_route.py. The model-free strip-summed column (FA's colsum) is the better estimator if it gets per-cell statistics.

Details

From the CAD (MX17_Geant design/GEOMETRY_FROM_CAD.md): the readout PCB is 1.70 mm, backed by 5 mm Rohacell, clamped between the 30 mm Al gas frame (410 mm aperture) and an 8 mm Al support plate with a 402 mm through-aperture, so nothing stiff sits behind the active area. Plate: FR4 E = 20 GPa, ν = 0.3, D = 9.0 N·m (Rohacell has ~1/300 of FR4's modulus and no second skin, so the PCB alone is taken), span 0.402 m. Square-plate coefficients (Timoshenko): centre deflection 0.00406 q a⁴/D simply supported, 0.00126 clamped.

Gas overpressure needed for 2 mm: 1.7 mbar (free) to 5.5 mbar (clamped); a bubbler-limited flow gives mbar-level overpressure. Gravity alone is 0.1–0.4 mm. A mesh laminated under 5–15 N/cm, acting on one face of the board, gives 0.4–1.2 mm over ±150 mm if the edges can rotate, ~0 if fully clamped. The measured 1.5–2.3 mm is inside this range, with gas pressure the only load that can reach it alone. At n_TOF the chambers stand vertical: the gravity part is gone, the pressure and tension parts are not.

Details

The angle map acts after the waveform fit (wft.reco.tan_of converts the fitted w and p0 into tan), so the gate is exact on the per-event tables: no refit is needed. Training half = even event ids, test = odd, then swapped. Coordinates are the production ones (the fit's own p0 + 15 mm × tan, both views), not M3's.

  • const: w0 and kw refit on the training half (today's bundle content).
  • map1d: w0(p) and the angle scale s(p) in 12 bins of the view's own coordinate (the ws_widthtilt angle_map, in wft/ but in no bundle). Median gain 18 %.
  • bow2d: w = A(x, y) + B·tan·(1 + s_x x + s_y y), A quadratic in both coordinates (9 numbers). Median gain 16 %.
  • phys: A = c0 + v·r_f·∂h/∂p with h(x, y) fitted to the training half's positions; only c0 and r_f come from the angles. Median gain 15 %.

The 1-D map wins on several Y views because it also carries a binned angle scale s(p) (a local v map, FA's subject), which the quadratic arms model only linearly. Position: subtracting tan·h(x, y) improves the position s68 by 4–15 % (right panel).

what this does not rule out, and what goes into the reconstruction

Not ruled out

· a common bow of the M3 planes adding to (or taking from) the position-route depth: the chamber's own dish is 0.5–1.3× the quoted number

· the mechanism: gas overpressure, mesh tension and weight all push the same way; no pressure or torque record exists

· what the boards do at n_TOF (vertical, other flow): the bench map must not be transferred without an in-situ check

· a direct gap measurement: the column route is limited by its own X–Y systematics

Into the bundle (spec to FE)

· per chamber: a static bow block, the height surface h(x, y) and the drift-tilt surface A(x, y) with its constant removed

· per run: only the rigid tilt (w0) and kw, as today

· tan_of uses both coordinates of the track; the mesh point of a track is (p0, z + h)

· gate: the held-out table gate here, then FE's full gate; bench only
