Is every burst timed from its flash?

2026-08-17 · follows the last 88 pulses, one burst at a time · live matching numbers on the match QA page

No product carries an uncorrected mis-tagged gamma flash. Across 281,437 beam bursts of the campaign, 48 have a burst time reference that is demonstrably not their flash. Only four of those are inside matched pulses, and all four are the ones already corrected on 16 August — they stay visible to this test because the mis-tag is in the DREAM data while the correction lives in the join. Everything else flagged was refused by the matching chain and already sits in the ledger as an unmatched pulse. The worry that prompted this — a dropped flash absorbed into a per-bunch correction, passing silently inside a healthy-looking product — does not occur anywhere in the campaign.

Why this was worth checking

bunch_join defines a burst's time base by its first trigger: that trigger is called the gamma flash and everything else in the burst is timed from it. The brute-force pass on 16 August found three bursts where that is false — the flash was never recorded, and the first scintillator single ~1 ms later was tagged in its place, putting the whole burst 1 ms off the n_TOF clock. All three failed their per-bunch clock fit and were caught as unmatched pulses, which is the benign outcome. The question was whether a burst with the same defect could have been matched anyway, its offset quietly absorbed, leaving a product whose per-event DREAM times are wrong while every QA check passes.

Both are taken against each sub-run's own medians — the gate width is a DAQ setting and the flash hit count depends on which chambers were live, so neither may become a campaign constant. The flag fires below 0.5× on gap1 or 0.35× on the hit count, and on either axis the bulk and the threshold are separated by an empty decade.

One thing had to be got right, and it was wrong on the first pass: only a pulse that delivered protons has a gamma flash at all. A no-beam burst's first event carries 1–3 hits, so in a sub-run that is mostly empty pulses the median is the median of bursts with no flash, and the healthy bursts come out 30× high and get flagged — 348 false positives, every one with a perfectly normal 1 ms gap and a 4,100-hit flash. The reference is now built from bursts the ledger says had beam, and only those bursts are judged.

Two things the sweep found on its own

Two of the three unpairable bursts are explained. run_102/stat090_0001 burst 0 and run_118/stat090_0019 burst 0 are the first burst of their sub-run, and their tagged flash carries 22 and 13 hits against ~4,080 — there is no gamma flash in the file at all. Their physics triggers are feeble too (median 23 hits against ~150), so the DAQ started recording partway through the gate and caught the weak tail of a burst. Their time base is anchored to a background trigger, which is why no bunch could be found for them; they are not merely small, and no scan would have paired them. The third, run_116/stat090_0028 burst 1073, has a perfectly normal flash (gap1 1.11 ms, 2,992 hits) and stays unexplained.

41 of the 1,516 “n_TOF not recording” pulses delivered no beam to the EAR2 target either. Their bursts have no gamma flash (23–794 hits) and feeble physics triggers — 14–38 hits each against 138–178 in the healthy bursts of the very same sub-run, at ~23 triggers per burst against ~90. They are interleaved with healthy bursts pulse by pulse rather than forming a block, so this is not a detector warming up at the start of a run: it is the PS pulse not arriving. The beam record says those pulses were extracted; DREAM says they did not reach this target; n_TOF's own PKUP would have settled it and n_TOF was not recording — the two absences agree. Nothing in the accounting moves, since the class is already outside the matching denominator, but it is a slightly stronger statement than the ledger makes: for those pulses there was nothing to match, not merely nothing recorded.

A hazard this turned up in yesterday's code

The per-burst overrides written on 16 August identify a bunch by number alone — and a bunch number means nothing without its n_TOF run, since every run numbers from 1. A sub-run that straddles a run boundary is joined once per n_TOF run, so the override would have fired in both, moving the burst onto a real but unrelated pulse of the other run. The existing guard could not catch it: that bunch genuinely exists there. No product was affected — only the correct segment happened to be re-made — but the entries are now keyed to their ntof_run and the slim refuses to apply one outside it, with a test for the case. This is the kind of thing a sweep finds and a spot check does not.

What this does not settle

  • Bursts of fewer than five triggers have no meaningful gap1 and are not judged; the ledger already counts them as noise clusters rather than pulses.
  • A flash that was recorded, correctly first, yet displaced in time by the DAQ would be caught by neither signature. Nothing in the campaign suggests that mode, but nothing here excludes it.
  • This is a statement about the time reference. The per-trigger timing resolution within a burst is a different question, and the clock fit's residuals are what measure it.
