Chamber A: the mesh, the cathode and the drift field

MX17 cosmic bench, chamber A (det3), the two long runs at the operating point (490 V mesh, 1000 V drift), final reconstruction (pass 1). 2026-11-24. Paper I, Sec. 5.1 and the appendix "The mesh and the cathode of chamber A".

What we found. The drift gap of chamber A is 28.2 mm on average, but it is not the same everywhere. It runs from about 27 to about 29 mm over the chamber. We describe the chamber with two surfaces: the mesh, which sits on the readout board, and the cathode. One model of these two surfaces describes every measurement we have: the positions, the drift direction, the geometric gap, the drift time and the drift velocity. The readout board is dished, and its dish is measured twice, once with the reference telescope and once without it. Along y the cathode stays nearly flat above the dished board, so the gap is longest in the middle. Along x the cathode follows the board, and the gap only increases slowly with x. Finally, a finer map of the drift direction improves the reconstructed angles of chamber A by 16 to 21 %.

2. What the geometry does to the drift field

The two surfaces change the drift field in two ways. First, the strength of the field is the drift voltage divided by the local gap. Where the gap is shorter, the field is stronger, the electrons drift faster, and they arrive sooner. Second, the direction of the field follows the slopes of the two surfaces. A tilted drift line moves the arriving charge sideways in proportion to its drift time. The reconstruction reads this sideways motion as part of the track angle. 3. The measurements

Each map below is measured directly, in 4 × 4 cells of the interior (cells of about 85 × 75 mm), with the two runs combined. Each map is shown as a change relative to its own chamber average, so that offsets between methods do not enter. Empty cells are outside the reach of that method: inclined reference tracks reach only the centre of the chamber in the direction they are inclined.

Three measurements of the gap

The geometric gap uses no timing. An inclined track crosses the gap over a length L along the strips, so the gap is L / tan θ. We measure it with the X strips and, independently, with the Y strips. The third measurement multiplies the angle-scale velocity (from the reference tracks) by the duration of the drift column in each cell. The field dependence cancels in this product, so it measures the gap too.

The field and the drift direction

The drift time alone and the velocity alone each depend on the gap through the field. Converted to a gap change, they show the same pattern. The mesh height comes from the positions of the tracks (the bow of section 10 of the paper). The rate offset w₀ is the sideways drift speed: it is the drift tilt times the drift velocity.

4. Why it is the gap and not the gas

A change of the gap and a change of the gas both change the drift time, but they change the drift velocity in different ways. At a fixed drift voltage a shorter gap means a stronger field, so the electrons drift faster and have less distance to go. A wetter gas only slows the drift. Across the cells, the logarithm of the velocity changes by −0.50 ± 0.02 times the logarithm of the drift time. A pure change of the gap predicts −0.41 (with the Magboltz slope β = 0.68), and a change of the gas predicts −1. The same pattern appears in two runs taken hours apart, which also argues against a local change of the gas.

5. The bow and the gap are the same two surfaces

The test of consistency is to fit the model to subsets of the observables and compare the surfaces they give. The table lists the depth of each surface at 150 mm from the centre (positive means the centre is lower). The drift direction measures the sum of the two slopes, mesh plus cathode, and the gap measures their difference. So together they determine the mesh without any position information ("no positions"). That fit finds the same dish as the position route.

  • Along y the cathode is nearly flat in every subset (−0.2 to 0.4 mm at 150 mm, against 1.4–1.6 mm for the mesh).
  • Along x the cathode follows the board. The one subset that disagrees is the one without timing, at 2.5σ. The geometric gap with the X strips reaches only x ≈ 100–320 mm, so it constrains this direction weakly.
  • The reference telescope is not dished. A separate dish of the telescope, added to the position route, fits to −0.25 ± 0.16 and −0.05 ± 0.20 mm.

6. Better tracking

The reconstruction already corrects the drift tilt with a quadratic surface (the bow block). The measured rate offset has more structure than a quadratic, about 0.18 µm/ns, which is about 0.3° in angle. To test whether this can be corrected, we make a map of the rate offset and the velocity on one run and apply it to the other run. The map is indexed by the reconstructed position of the track, and only two constants are measured again on the test run. Since the map and the test use different runs, the gain cannot come from fitting the noise of the test sample.

angle resolution (s68 of θ − θ_ref)
Filled: Run 1, open: Run 2. Solid: X strips, dashed: Y strips. Left: pass 1. Each map is made on the other run.
42 mm cells improve the angles by 0.15–0.17°, which is 16–21 %, on both views and in both runs. Most of the gain comes from the offset. With 34 mm cells the map made on the smaller Run 1 becomes noisy.

The structure the map corrects is two-dimensional and smooth. It is not a timing offset between front-end chips. A chip reads 64 strips (50 mm), so a chip offset would show steps along the view's own coordinate, and there are none. A map along that coordinate alone recovers only a fifth to a third of the gain. We therefore attribute the structure to the shape of the drift field on scales of about 50 mm, finer than a quadratic surface can follow.

7. What this does not show

  • The size of the dome along y is known to about 30 %. The drift-time product shows about 0.7 of the amplitude of the geometric gap.
  • The fitted β is 1.0 ± 0.2, against 0.68 from Magboltz and 0.87 from the drift-time scan. The surfaces do not depend on this value.
  • The drift tilt along x rises faster toward the edges than a quadratic dish allows. The board is not a pure paraboloid, and the rf along x is window-dependent.
  • Only one mounting of chamber A at the operating point is usable. The mean gap, 1.8 mm short of the 30 mm design, is not explained.
  • Nothing here has been tested at n_TOF. There the boards stand vertical, so the part of the bow caused by gravity is absent, and there are no reference tracks.
  • The map is not in the production reconstruction. Shipping it is an open decision.
