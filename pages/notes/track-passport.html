1

Details

The question (Dylan, 15 Oct). The MM dE/dx study and the scintillator calorimetry each worked on their own. Combined, how much can this set-up say about a single track? The answer here is meant as a demonstrator: what a TPC-plus-scintillator stack can measure, with numbers, so that a future set-up (LNL or elsewhere) can be designed from it. It is not an X17 analysis.

Structure. Part 1, the principle: what each layer measures and why (Heed and Geant4 truth). Part 2, how it should work, in a full simulation with the measured resolutions. Part 3, the real detector: calibration, what the real chamber adds, validation against Geant4, example tracks, and the performance table.

Sample. run_145 (5 Aug 2026, the post-23-July noisy configuration), chambers A and C, ≥ 12 ms after the γ-flash, single gated tracks on their own arm's trigger (wall AND plastic). Cosmic checks use run_149 (beam off). Geant4: the thermal-neutron campaign on the 2 cm capsule nose (66,603 tracks crossing a gap, 5,726 triggered).

Code: ntof_calorimetry/track_passport.py, make_passport_deck.py; MM chain in mm_edep_*.py, record in ntof_calorimetry/MM_EDEP.md.

2

Details

Geometry (scint-stack geometry.csv): the wall is 97 mm behind the mesh, the plastic 190 mm, the liquid 250 mm. The MM measures x and y with two strip views and the depth z from the drift time; together they give the 3D direction of the track in the gap, and so where it crosses each counter and how long its path through it is.

Why combine. A single number per layer is ambiguous: a large plastic signal could be a fast electron at a steep angle or a slow one that stopped. The track angle resolves that. A large gas signal could be a slow electron or two particles. The plastic resolves that (a slow electron does not reach it). Each layer removes an ambiguity of another.

Principle 1: a thin gas layer measures dE/dx, not energy

ionisation left by one particle crossing 30 mm of Ar/iC4H10 at normal incidence (Heed truth)

Above ~1 MeV an electron leaves 5.3–6.5 keV in the gap whatever its energy (+22 % from 1 to 16 MeV): the gas cannot weigh a fast electron.

Below ~0.5 MeV the deposit climbs steeply: a slow electron stands out.

What the gas can count is particles: two fast electrons leave twice as much.

One keV ≈ 36 ion pairs. The MM collects them and the avalanche makes them visible.

3

Details

Source. Garfield++/Heed on lxplus (heed_edep.py), 20,000 tracks per point, the n_TOF gas (Ar/iC4H10 90/10, 730 Torr), 30 mm, straight tracks. Energy = number of ionisation electrons × 27.5 eV.

The physics. Bethe-Bloch: dE/dx falls as 1/β² for slow particles, reaches a minimum near βγ ≈ 3 and then rises slowly (the relativistic rise, about +24 % in the MPV from 1.5 to 16 MeV here, the same as a 3 GeV muon). A 1 MeV electron already has βγ ≈ 2.8, so every electron the X17 set-up cares about is on the plateau.

Consequence for the design. The gas layer is a counter of ionising particles and a slow-particle tag, not a calorimeter. Energy has to come from a layer thick enough to stop the electron.

Principle 2: one dE/dx sample is broad, but two particles still stand out

distribution of the gap deposit, Heed truth, normal incidence, 30 mm

one 4 MeV electron

two of them

cosmic muon

A single sample fluctuates a lot: FWHM ≈ 0.6 × the peak, with a long Landau tail.

Two particles shift the whole distribution up ×2; they overlap the one-particle tail only in part.

An electron and a muon look the same: both are minimum-ionising. So cosmic muons can calibrate the chamber for beam electrons.

4

Details

Why it is broad. 30 mm of gas sees only ~30 primary collisions; their number is Poisson and each one's energy has a 1/E² spectrum, so a few close collisions dominate some tracks. The result is the asymmetric Landau-like shape with the long high-side tail. A thin plastic layer (the wall) has the same shape, narrower, because it sees thousands of collisions.

What it means for "two particles". Separation is statistical, not event-by-event perfect: the 2-particle distribution peaks at twice the single one but the single one's tail reaches into it. Simulation and data later quantify it as an AUC.

Principle 3: the track tells every layer how much material the particle crossed

a slab of thickness d crossed at angle θ: the deposit scales with the path d · sec θ

5

Details

This is the simplest link between tracking and calorimetry: the MM measures θ in its gap, and each counter's expected deposit is (deposit at normal incidence) × sec θ. Heed's muon deposit follows sec θ slightly faster than linear (the MPV per unit length grows with thickness), slope 1.07 in log–log.

Two places it matters: (1) the gas dE/dx, normalised to normal incidence, is comparable across tracks; (2) a particle that goes through the 20 mm plastic leaves MIP × sec θ = 3.4 × sec θ MeV, so without the angle a steep through-going electron looks like a slower stopping one. Part 2 shows the angle buys back energy resolution for exactly those tracks.

Principle 4: the 20 mm plastic is a calorimeter, up to the energy that gets through it

Geant4 neutron-beam events: plastic deposit (all particles) against the leading lepton's T at the gap

Up to ~5 MeV the electron stops in the 20 mm plastic: the deposit is its energy, minus ~1.2 MeV lost in the windows, the wall and the air on the way.

Above ~6 MeV it gets through: the deposit saturates at MIP × path (~3.4–4 MeV), and the liquid behind it fires.

So the plastic measures energy only up to ~6 MeV; above that it says "fast", and the angle sets the expected plateau.

6

Details

Sample. Geant4 thermal-neutron events on the capsule nose (stack_edep.py), every track crossing a gap with any energy in the matched plastic bar. The deposit is the total prompt energy in the bar (the lepton plus its deltas and bremsstrahlung). The band is the 16–84 % range of tracks per bin.

Range. The CSDA range of a 4 MeV electron in PVT is about 2 cm; with ~1.2 MeV lost before the plastic (C2), electrons up to ~5 MeV at the gap stop inside. This is why the plastic is linear 3–8 MeV for stopping electrons (the scintillator calorimetry note) but cannot measure an X17 leg above ~6 MeV on its own.

Design lesson. A future calorimeter for 8–16 MeV legs needs ≥ 5–8 cm of plastic (or a denser crystal), segmented so that the track picks the cell.

7

Details

The chain (mm_edep_sim.py, mm_charge.py, mm_edep_ana.py): the ionisation drifts to the mesh, is amplified, and is read on x and y strips in 20 samples of 60 ns. The estimator sums the pedestal- and common-mode-subtracted charge in a road around the reconstructed track (both views), divides by the share of the charge the window holds, c(f), and by k (ADC per keV, from the simulation).

The window. f = (1200 ns − t0) / (30 mm / v) is the fraction of the drift that fits in the readout. In A (v ≈ 34 µm/ns) the whole drift fits for almost every track; in C (v ≈ 26 µm/ns, a slower gas mixture/field) it does not, and the last ~20 % of the track is extrapolated. That is the main reason C is less precise (16 % vs 10 % in simulation). Design lesson: size the window to the slowest drift, with margin for t0.

Putting it together: four questions, each answered by a different combination

8

Details

Each question has a natural measurement, and each measurement needs the tracking. The pointing is pure tracking. The particle count is the gas deposit divided by the path length the track gives. The stopped/through decision compares the plastic deposit with what a through-going particle would leave along this track's path (3.4 MeV × sec θ), backed by the liquid. The energy uses everything. In the code (track_passport.apply_to_data) the last three are k-nearest-neighbour estimators trained on Geant4; the class labels shown on the passports are simple cuts on their outputs.

10

Details

A thermal neutron captured in the capsule's aluminium emits a γ cascade up to 7.7 MeV; the γ Compton-scatters or converts in the capsule wall and the lepton flies to the arm. The trigger (a wall group AND a plastic bar above threshold) keeps leptons that reach the plastic, i.e. above ~2.5–3 MeV at the gap.

These are the truths the measurements will be asked to recover: T (energy), several particles in the gap, pair conversion vs Compton, capsule vs elsewhere. e⁺ vs e⁻ cannot be recovered at all without a magnetic field.

11

Details

Why the wall cannot see it. The second particle is usually soft: a delta electron, an Auger/photo electron, or the low-energy partner of a Compton or pair process. It ranges out within centimetres of gas or material and never reaches the wall 97 mm away. The MM samples the particles exactly where they are produced together.

AUC is the probability that a random multi-particle track scores higher than a random single one (0.5 = no information, 1 = perfect). 0.84 means the gas deposit alone ranks multi-particle tracks well but not perfectly (the single-particle Landau tail overlaps, slide 4).

12

Details

Why the angle helps. A steep track that gets through the plastic leaves 3.4 × sec θ MeV, which the plastic alone cannot tell from a slower electron stopping in it; given θ, the estimator knows which plateau to expect. Left: without the angle (dashed) the energy width grows with the track angle; with it (solid) it stays flat. Middle: the width against the true energy; above ~6 MeV every feature set loses because the electron leaves the plastic.

The MM's share. It adds nothing for fast tracks (the gas is minimum-ionising, slide 3), but helps the slow ones (T < 3 MeV: 1.56 → 1.35 MeV) whose gas deposit is rising. For tracks that never reach the plastic, the MM halves the vertex-energy width of 1–3.5 MeV capsule electrons (1.7 → 0.45–0.8 MeV; mm_edep_combo C2 study).

Ceiling. Above ~6 MeV every set loses: the electron leaves the plastic. That is the 20 mm thickness, not the method.

Details

Calibration. The ADC-per-keV constant k and the window curve come from the beam simulation, generated at the beam gain (A 2471, C 1233 ADC/keV); the earlier cosmic k left a −0.46 bias on beam. The data/sim gain is a forward fold of the data distribution onto the simulation's (scale × a Gaussian extra spread σ_x), fitted on the core: A 1.226 (σ_x 0.13), C 1.163 (σ_x 0.01).

Result. The median deposit is 5.68 / 5.65 keV per 30 mm in the truth the data are calibrated to; the data's shape (peak, width, Landau tail) is the simulation's. Per-track resolution = simulation chain ⊕ σ_x: A 16 %, C 22 %.

Caveat. This calibration is relative to the simulation's electron spectrum; an absolute check needs a known source in the gas (e.g. ⁵⁵Fe) — a recommendation for the next set-up.

Details

The finding. Two tracks within 4 mm of each other have correlated residuals (r = 0.43 A, 0.33 C) and the correlation dies out over 20–40 mm. Pairs at the same x but far apart in y stay correlated (0.26) in both the x and y views; pairs at the same y do not (0.01). So it is an amplification-gap structure along x, not channel electronics. The calibration bundles have all channel gains at exactly 1.0, so the simulation has none of it.

The map. For each track, the median residual of its 25 nearest neighbours (cross-validated: never its own fold). After it, the correlation is gone. The cosmic map does not transfer to beam (different gain), so the beam needs its own map.

Halves test. Split each contained track's drift in two halves; their common excess over the simulation is a per-track gain fluctuation, measured without any truth. After the map: 0.27 (A cosmics), 0.09 (beam).

Real chamber, step 3: the γ-flash blinds the gas measurement for ~10 ms

median gas deposit (normalised to ≥ 12 ms) against time since the flash

The flash leaves the chamber at ~30 % gain for 8 ms; it recovers by 12 ms and is flat (±3 %) to 80 ms.

Before ~9 ms the x and y views disagree (x/y 0.2–0.7): the charge is not a deposit measurement there.

Lesson: a beam set-up needs a flash-proof MM (gating, a lower field during the flash) or a monitored gain.

Details

How. The estimators (k-nearest neighbours in plastic, wall, sec θ, gas deposit) are trained on the Geant4 triggered tracks and applied to each data track after its counter energies are put on Geant4's scale. The Geant4 curves are the same estimators, cross-validated, so they include the estimators' own smearing.

Stopped early = plastic deposit below 0.8 × MIP × sec θ: a lepton that stopped in the plastic well below the through-going plateau. C has many more (its geometry); Geant4 predicts that. The liquid fires behind 4 % of A tracks and almost never behind C's (C's MIP is only ~1.3 × its zero-suppression threshold).

Mean P(multi) in the data vs the Geant4 truth fraction is a consistency check only: P is a probability from the simulation, so its mean is the simulation's multi-particle rate as seen through the data.

What this demonstrator says for the next set-up

each item measured on the n_TOF data or its simulation

Thicker, segmented calorimeter

the 20 mm plastic stops only ≤ 5–6 MeV electrons; X17 legs reach 8–16 MeV. ≥ 5–8 cm of plastic or a crystal, segmented so the track picks the cell.

Readout window sized to the drift

C's drift runs past the 1200 ns window: 16 % vs 10 % in simulation. Window ≥ 1.4 × the slowest full drift.

Gain uniformity and in-situ calibration

a 1D gain structure along x needed a track-based map; an ⁵⁵Fe or X-ray source in the gas would give an absolute keV scale.

Flash / rate robustness

the MM is blind for ~10 ms after the flash and the PMTs sag 3–8 %: gate the MM, use rate-proof bases or a pulser monitor.

Keep the gas dE/dx

it is the only layer that counts the particles where they are made (AUC 0.84): the tag for a two-track overlap or a δ-ray.

A magnetic field, if charge matters

without one, e⁺ and e⁻ are indistinguishable; with one, the TPC gives momentum and sign.

The combined stack works as a particle-by-particle detector; the limits are thickness, window and calibration, not the method

what the data show

  • Each layer measures what the physics says it should: dE/dx in the gas and the wall, energy in the plastic for stopping electrons, penetration in the liquid.
  • The tracking ties them together: path length (muons follow sec θ), energy for steep tracks, pointing to the source.
  • The gas is the only particle counter; it works on real data as Geant4 predicts.
  • Per-track gas dE/dx: 16 % (A) / 22 % (C) on beam, after an in-situ gain map.

what this does not rule out

  • Energies and particle probabilities are Geant4 estimators: a wrong Geant4 scale moves the data with it.
  • "Several particles" is a Geant4 definition; its data rate is inferred, not measured.
  • The MM keV scale is relative to the simulation, not to an absolute source.
  • No liquid in the Geant4 sample; only 3–8 MeV electrons tested (the trigger sets the floor); sub-thermal neutrons only.
