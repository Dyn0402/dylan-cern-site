On the flank the beam meets the wall at ~65°. A film of fixed normal thickness is ×2.4 longer along the beam, the protons leave it below 441 keV and 29–71 % of the γ₀ moves to 17.64 MeV.

New on 11 Nov: a 20 µm lithium film is not safe against LiOH, 10–12 µm is

Residual water turns Li into LiOH. The lithium atoms stay, but the O and H add stopping, so the beam slows down to the strong 441 keV resonance inside the film. This design assumed 20 µm

Details

TARGET_REVIEW.md (11 Nov 2026) §4.1, §4.4, §6.3, and the note at the top of CU_LINER.md (its §8 keeps the 4 Nov text as the record). Cu on Al: straight onto the oxide it adheres poorly, and no adhesion or stress data were found above 10 µm. The route with a precedent is a 1–3 µm sputtered seed with a Ti or Cr adhesion layer, then electroplating (CERN’s Cu on Al sequence). Uniform along the normal means ×2.4 more Cu on the flank than needed, which costs nothing measurable. Plated version simulated at 0.3 mm. The lithium: a flat foil pressed into a tip of R 2.8 mm stretches and thins unevenly. A disc that stays on the tip cap (r ≤ 2.5 mm) takes 55 % of a σ 2 mm beam. A rounder tip (R 6–8 mm) would take 95 % and is a question for Pierfrancesco. Margins (CU_LINER.md §5): 1 bar ×4.8 (0.3 mm) / ×10 (0.4 mm), tip +54–69 K at 4 µA. Hydrogen in the Cu is a watch item, not a blocker: MEG II stopped 8–11 µA in 25 µm of Cu for four weeks without significant deterioration. A plated-through layer is safer than a 12 µm film, where a 6 µm blister cap is half the film. Not a loose Cu cup: no thermal contact and a trapped volume.
