On 11 Nov 2026 a buildability review (lnl/target/TARGET_REVIEW.md) checked every design against how lithium targets are really made, at LNL and elsewhere. This deck is the comparison of 27 Oct brought in line with it. Earlier designs stay as a record, each with its verdict.

Days are days of beam at 1 µA (the documented current at 1.0–1.1 MeV) to reach 3σ at the ATOMKI strength (R = 5.8 × 10⁻⁶), E_p = 1.10 MeV, m_X = 16.8 MeV. They come from the reference fit, θ × |y| × E_sum with the background shape floating, made safe against thin Monte Carlo (nd_reach.csv, FEASIBILITY_SIM.md §19; interim until run L13 is folded in). Three detector states: MX17 with the big plastics, the detector as built with the liquid scintillator repaired, and as built with the liquid as it ran at n_TOF. As built assumes a 0.3 ns arm-to-arm time of flight that no measurement backs; with track pointing alone the liquid-as-at-n_TOF value is about 20 d for the Cu ogive (FEASIBILITY_SIM.md §19.4).

"Material per lepton" is the median x/X₀ crossed by an X17 lepton on its way to the arms, from one ray-trace applied to every design in the same way (opt_sketch.py). It ranks the designs. It is not Geant4.

The review (lnl/target/TARGET_REVIEW.md, 11 Nov 2026) in one table. The verdicts come from lnl/target/review_data.py and the days from nd_reach.csv (reference fit). A range covers the simulated variants of a design. For a dropped family it covers every simulated design in it.

Flat aluminium end cap at 45°: proposed. One piece of aluminium with a flat face at 45°, a flat Cu stop and rolled Li pressed on with flat moulds. Every step is something LNL or any workshop already does. The Li is uniform along the beam by geometry, so there is no flank, no mask and no line-of-sight coating.

Cu ogive: buildable now. Electroform it in hard copper (a 200 µm annealed shell has only ×2–3 on buckling). Pressing a rolled foil into a tip of R 2.8 mm needs new moulds, and Li that overlaps the 65° flank is ×2.4 thicker along the beam.

Al ogive, Cu plated over the whole inside: buildable now. Al 0.4 mm with Cu plated over the whole inside (sputtered seed, then electroplating: the route with a precedent), Li pressed on the tip. No sputtering deep in the cone, no masks, no lithium evaporator.

Cu cup: buildable now. The CN neutron-line target: 20 µm of rolled Li pressed on a 300 µm Cu cup. It works today and is the commissioning target, but every lepton crosses the copper.

Al ogive + Cu patch + masked Li: possible, a development project. A 0.35 mm wall turned 63 mm deep, ≥ 12 µm of Cu stuck to aluminium at the bottom of the cone, and Li evaporated through a mask from far out on the axis (0.8–5 g of lithium per coating). With today’s detector it gains ×1.0–1.7 on the Cu ogive; the ×2.4–3.5 needs the big plastics.

Ti stop: possible, a development project. Titanium takes up hydrogen without blistering and makes no x-rays that escape, but nobody has tested a 15 µm Ti film under MeV protons and evaporated Ti has peeled from smooth substrates.

Beryllium: dropped. The last supplier of machined and welded beryllium vacuum tubes stopped in 2023 (CERN now makes its own). A custom thin shell is 20–60 k€ and 6–12 months (estimate), plus a safety case, and all the Be designs carry the Mo stop.

Molybdenum stops: dropped. The Geant4 x-ray check of 4 Nov doubled the Mo K yield: 19–45 % Micromegas occupancy per arm per µA, and no thin wall absorbs 17.5 keV. These designs would have to run at 0.2–0.5 µA.

The diamond: dropped. At 1 µA the beam is 1.1 W; a 0.4 mm aluminium face carries it with a 5 K rise. The diamond is brittle, has never been hydrogen-tested, costs 6–15 k€ mounted (estimate), and carries the Mo stop. The 45° geometry is kept, in aluminium.

Thin LiF / Li₂O films: dropped. The AN2000 group ran 24 thin LiF films on self-supporting backings: most gave 6–8 h of stable rate, the best two days; their one Li₂O film broke at once. Hanoi’s films were "punctured many times".

The thin-film row is the ATOMKI-style baseline (a 300 µg/cm² Li₂O film on 10 µm of Al in a chamber, beam to a dump). It has no slide of its own here. As built assumes a 0.3 ns arm-to-arm time of flight that no measurement backs; with track pointing alone the liquid-as-at-n_TOF value is about 20 d for the Cu ogive (FEASIBILITY_SIM.md §19.4).

No laboratory was found that deposits Li, Cu or Ti inside the beam pipe. Li metal is always made off the line (glovebox, or an evaporation stand with a vacuum transfer). A 20 µm film does not need more: a few nm to ~0.5 µm of surface compound from the transfer is ≤ 3 % of it.

20 µm of lithium metal on copper is LNL’s routine. That is exactly our film and our stop, made without any evaporation.

Thin compound films on thin backings are what the pair experiments chose. At LNL they broke in hours. A film on a metal stop that carries the heat is the robust layout.

Details

Half of a 20 µm Li film turned to LiOH by residual water takes a 1.10 MeV beam down to 454 keV: 52 % of the γ₀ then comes from the 441 keV resonance (fresh 0.85 %; 91 % at 1.03 MeV). 10–12 µm of Li stays at or below 10 % even fully converted, for 51–61 % of the yield. The rate is unknown: 1.35 µm a day at 10⁻⁷ mbar of water if every molecule sticks (an upper limit; LiOH passivates). A one-week film test on the LNL line decides the thickness.

What it means: the 441 keV resonance is about 50 times stronger than the 18.15 MeV one. Once the beam reaches it inside the film, the 17.64 MeV γ-rays and their pairs become most of what the detector sees, the trigger rate rises several-fold, and the 18.15 MeV signal sits under a background that was not in the design. This is the ATOMKI 2016 accident by another route. It is a property of the film, not of the shell, so it applies to every design here.

What to do (TARGET_REVIEW.md §4.2): make the film 10–12 µm, which LNL already rolls ("down to sub-10 µm"). Run at 1.10 MeV, not 1.03, for 70 keV more margin. Watch the γ₀ yield per µC, which rises by several times as the beam reaches 441 keV. Measure the rate before the run with one film on the AN2000 or CN line for a week. Keep the cold trap upstream, and no PVC next to the target if it can be avoided.

Model: review_li_conversion.py → out/target/options/review_li_conversion.csv (the repo’s yield model, PSTAR stopping, the outer fraction of the Li atoms converted and the rest metal). With Li₂O instead of LiOH the effect is much smaller at 1.10 MeV. The thin-film days fold the lower yield and the changed line mix into the reference fit (nd_reach_thinli.csv). They are shown for the configurations that have rows.

What must change before a target is built: thinner lithium, hard copper, a 0.4 mm aluminium wall

Closed-form shell, plate and fin estimates (ESTIMATE). The LNL mechanical office should confirm by FEA

Every design: thinner lithium

Half of a 20 µm film turned to hydroxide puts 52 % of the γ₀ on the 441 keV resonance. A 12 µm film stays at 10 % even fully converted, for 61 % of the yield. A one-week film test at LNL decides the thickness.

Cu ogive: hard copper

At 200 µm, soft annealed copper buckles at 3.0 bar, just under CERN’s margin of 3. Electroform it in hard copper on an aluminium mandrel, or make it 0.25 mm. Press the Li on the tip cap only.

Al ogives: the wall

Al 200 µm buckles at 1.8 bar, 300 µm at 4.9. Build 0.4 mm: it also stops the Cu K x-rays. Turned 6061 is comfortable at 0.5–0.8 mm, so plan one or two scrapped parts.

The flat cap: the face

Over a Ø20 bore, 0.4 mm of Al 6061-T6 carries 1 bar at 80 MPa (margin ×3.5), 0.5 mm at 51 MPa (×5.4). 0.3 mm is too thin (×1.9). Coat the copper cool: 6061-T6 softens above about 200 °C.

Heat is not the limit anywhere

At 1 µA the beam is 1.1 W. The cap’s face rises 5 K, and no design passes 62 K even at 4 µA. This is why the diamond is not needed.

Hydrogen in copper: watch it

The "blisters after 5 days" came from much higher current densities. MEG II stopped 8–11 µA in 25 µm of Cu for four weeks without damage. A 12 µm Cu film is the weak case, so prefer plated-through or foil copper, and plan 2–3 spares.
