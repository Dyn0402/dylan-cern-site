Details

This deck answers one question: is the det3 X left/right neighbour asymmetry (NB_ASYM.md) a real detector effect, or something the analysis made? It starts from the most basic observable there is and builds up.

Everything here was recomputed from the decoded DREAM files with a new extractor, raw_asym.py, that shares no code with the earlier analysis (no FeuReader, no strip map, no common-mode subtraction). The earlier copy survey is used only where noted.

Short answer: yes, it is real, it is a copy of the peak channel’s own pulse into its upper channel neighbour, it follows the board (not the FEU slot, not the view), it is strongest in the middle of the board, and it changes with drift field. Its cause is not established.

What is measured: a cosmic, a few pads, three channels

Hover the dotted terms and any data point for definitions, counts and sources.

2

Details

A cosmic muon ionises the ~27 mm drift gap; electrons drift to the mesh, avalanche in the 150 µm gap, and induce signal on the readout pads under the resistive layer. The pads form a checkerboard: every other pad belongs to an X channel, the others to a Y channel (gerbers: 131 072 stubs each). Each pad reaches its channel’s bus line through a via and a 390 µm stub, and every stub of a layer points the same way, so each bus runs under the inter-pad gap on one side of its pads. That is the only built-in left/right asymmetry of the board we know of; slide 18 asks how large an effect it can make.

The readout: 512 channels per view, 64-channel connectors and DREAM chips, 32 samples of 60 ns per channel per trigger. These runs had no zero suppression, no on-board pedestal and no common-noise subtraction (run_config dream_daq_info).

Throughout: p is the channel with the largest pulse; p−1 and p+1 are its neighbours in DAQ channel number. With the connector-inverted wiring of all these runs, channel p+1 sits at the lower strip position.

Details

Your prior is the right one: nothing in the physics of a micromegas prefers one side. Cosmics arrive from both sides, diffusion and the avalanche are isotropic, and the resistive-strip/pad beat averages to zero over the board (NB_ASYM §1: peak rank mod 40 shows no modulation, sine amplitude 0.020 ± 0.024).

So a left/right difference has to come either from the hardware layout (the bus traces are one-sided), from the readout (adjacent pins, the order the ADC reads channels), or from the analysis. The deck tests each in turn and comes back to this chain at the end with a verdict per box.

5

Details

Every step is symmetric between p−1 and p+1: a per-channel constant, an argmax over channels, and two array lookups. A per-channel gain difference cannot create a net asymmetry either, because p runs over all channels and each channel is p−1 for one peak and p+1 for another.

The cuts act on the peak channel only and never look at the neighbours, except the isolation (no other channel over 5σ beyond p ± 3), which is symmetric.

Hot channels: a channel that fires on noise produces “events” with p always the same channel, so its two fixed neighbours decide the left/right answer. The veto removes a channel that is the peak more than six times the median rate of the channels that ever are. On det3 June X it removes channel 492 (6-23/24 runs).

Sources: raw_asym.py, asym_lib.py

Details

This is the simplest observable I can think of: for each event, is the right neighbour (channel p+1) bigger than the left (p−1)? It needs no calibration, no model and no strip map. A detector with no left/right preference gives 50 % within the binomial error.

The May build of det3, read through the same FEU 3, is at 50 %. det4 and det7 X (built in Stephan’s June-22 batch) sit a few % high. The weekend det3 run is lower because its neighbours are wider (a copy of the same absolute size changes the ranking less).

The excess is a 23 % copy of the peak’s own pulse

det3 June-15 X, 6-24 long run · samples where the peak pulse is above 20 % of its maximum

9

Details

If the extra charge on p+1 were real induced charge from a displaced cloud, or a crosstalk through a capacitor (which differentiates), it would have its own shape and timing. Instead the mean difference (p+1) − (p−1) is the peak channel’s own pulse times a constant, sample by sample, including the long tail.

NB_ASYM §1 fitted d = a·c + b·dc/dt on the earlier tagged dumps: b ≈ 0, residual rms 0.002–0.010.

So the cleanest description is: channel p+1 receives a fixed fraction of whatever channel p carries, at the same time. The next slide tests that “copy” description on the second neighbours.

Details

The copy model has one parameter. Undoing it runs upward through the channel window: x′(n+1) = x(n+1) − a·x′(n). Fitting a so that the first neighbours balance gives a = 0.192. The second neighbours were not used in the fit; raw they are p−2 0.0236 vs p+2 0.0347, after undoing 0.0224 vs 0.0086.

NB_ASYM §1 found the same on the tagged dumps (a from ±1 and from ±2 agree on every run: 0.085/0.085, 0.145/0.142, 0.196/0.167), and uncopy.py closes the full-FEU version (6-22: ±1 0.153 → 0.005).

In-phase content α_k = Σ_t x_k(t)·x_p(t) / Σ_t x_p(t)²: the part of channel p+k shaped like the peak pulse.

11

Details

Every DREAM channel has its own preamplifier noise. If anything after the input (shaper, multiplexer, ADC settling, a cable between FEU stages) added a × channel n to channel n+1, the noise would be copied too, and the correlation between neighbours (d = 1) would jump relative to d = 2. Injecting x(n+1) += 0.15 x(n) into these same quiet samples shows the size of that jump (dashed).

The measured X correlation has no d = 1 spike, the same as its Y view and as the May build (same FEU 3, no copy). The correlations themselves are common-mode residue; their level differs between boards and runs, so compare the shape (d = 1 vs d = 2), not the level.

Caveat: noise injected at the preamp input (its series noise) appears on the input node; coupling between input lines could carry some of it. So this test excludes the FEU chain, not a coupling on the input traces or connector. That one is addressed by “follows the board” (next slide) and by the field dependence (slide 17).

NB_ASYM had the same result with the older dumps (+0.03/−0.03/−0.02 vs +0.06…0.12 for an injected 0.1 copy).

13

Details

Each DREAM chip reads 64 adjacent X channels, about 50 mm of the board. The copy is ~0.1 at the two ends (chips 0, 1, 7) and 0.2–0.3 in chips 2–5, with the same shape on every run and at both drift fields. Chip order follows the board’s x position (the connectors sit in order along the edge).

This matters for the cause. A layout feature of the PCB (the bus traces, the connector pin order) repeats identically for every channel and every chip, so it would give a flat profile. A profile that peaks in the middle points to something that varies over the board’s area: the mesh or drift-gap geometry (a bowed mesh or cathode changes the local field), the bulk (lamination, pillars, coverlay thickness, curing), or moisture.

A threshold, saturation or a non-linear ADC effect would make the copy depend on the pulse height. It is flat within errors from 200 to 3500 ADC: a linear coupling.

NB_ASYM also found it flat in track angle (a real tilt gives −0.0085 per degree on det2; det3’s copy would need ~20°), flat in channel-within-connector and parity, and flat in the 39:40 resistive-strip beat phase.

A real trap: a hot channel faked a “turn-on” one evening

det3 June-15 X, 6-23 evening → 6-24 morning (same HV) · copy per data file, with and without the hot-channel veto

15

Details

The earlier survey (copy_survey.csv) showed det3’s copy at 0.035 at 19:29, 0.057 at 20:29 and 0.17 overnight, at unchanged HV, which looked like a slow turn-on. It is not. In the short run 3259 of 4493 “events” are channel 492 firing on noise (flat waveforms, no pulse); its two fixed neighbours decide the answer, and they happen to be nearly equal. As the run goes on, real cosmics outnumber it and the mean climbs to the true value.

With the pulse-shape cut and the hot-channel veto every file of the evening gives the same copy, and the per-chip profile of the longer run equals the long run’s.

This is exactly the kind of algorithm effect worth worrying about, and it shows the veto is needed before any time-dependence claim. The survey slides below use the raw re-extraction with the veto.

Not constant: it rises as the drift field drops

det3 June-15 X · raw re-extraction, hot channels vetoed · colour: drift voltage (dark low, yellow high)

17

Details

Left: every bench sub-run of det3’s June-15 build in X, at its start time. It is highest on 6-22/24 (FEU 3) and lower on 6-27/28 (FEU 7). The step coincides with the slot change, so time and slot are confounded here; the drift scan on the right is within one slot and one evening.

Right: the 6-27 drift scan ran from 1100 V down to 100 V in time order; the copy rises as the field drops. The long run that started right after, at 1000 V, returns to the 1000 V value: the field dependence is reversible, not a drift in time.

Middle: the mesh HV scans are flat up to ~500 V. Above it most peaks rail (cut), the surviving samples are small and several hot channels are vetoed, so the points there are few and noisy; they are not evidence of a gain dependence.

Why this matters: a cable, a connector or the FEU cannot know the drift field. A detector-side effect can: the drift field sets the field just above the mesh and the electron transparency, and any charge sitting on insulating surfaces near the pads responds to it.

Details

Each X channel is every other pad along a line; its pads reach a bus line through 390 µm stubs that all point the same way, so the bus lies under the gap on one side of the channel’s own pads, next to one neighbour. Charge landing near that gap induces a little on the bus too: a one-sided, prompt, linear copy. The pad kernels used in the simulation drive the pads only, so they are symmetric by construction.

The solve (bus_wpot.py): own pads, vias, rings, stubs and bus at 1 V, everything else grounded, read at the resistive layer and folded with a 300–450 µm cloud. With the Geant4 stack estimate (264 µm FR4 per layer) it gives 0.010, about what det1/det2/det6 show. It reaches 0.085 only with the bus 15 µm below the pads, which no 8-layer board has.

Not checked yet, and decisive: whether the side the buses sit on predicts the observed direction (p+1 gets the copy). That needs the gerber → strip-map orientation (board rotated 90° on the bench, connectors inverted). If it is the wrong side, the bus is out entirely. Also open: which data view sits on L5 vs L6; the solve says the L6 view should see ~0.001.

Sources: ../bus_wpot.py, ../bus_scan/d10_*.json, ../gerber_stubs.py

19

Details

Excluded with evidence here: the code (raw extraction, symmetric steps, 13 control views at 50 %), the FEU and ADC (same slot clean on other boards; noise not copied), track geometry (Y clean, flat in angle), the resistive-strip beat (flat in phase).

Too small alone: the bus traces (0.010 nominal). They are still the natural reason the copy has a direction at all, if something raises the coupling in the bulk (moisture or uncured material in the June bulk, charge on insulating surfaces near the gaps). That is speculation until a test separates it.

Not excluded: a fault in det3’s own X cable/adapter that travelled with it. Against it: the copy varies across the board chip by chip in a smooth, centre-peaked way, and with the drift field.

what this does not rule out, and what would settle it

The effect is real and belongs to det3’s June-15 board; its mechanism is open

Not ruled out

· det3’s X cable or adapter, if it moved with the board between FEU slots.

· The May and June-15 “det3” being different PCBs (then “re-bulking switched it on” is unproven).

· That the bus side predicts the opposite direction: not yet checked.

· The beam data (run_79, 0.05–0.07) rest on the older, non-raw survey.

Tests that would settle it

1. Swap det3’s X cable with another board’s: does the copy stay with det3?

2. Pin the gerber → channel orientation; does the bus side match p+1?

3. Ask Stephan what differed in the June-15 bulk (process, drying, coverlay).

4. A pulser into one pad of det3 X with HV off: copy present without gas gain or field?
