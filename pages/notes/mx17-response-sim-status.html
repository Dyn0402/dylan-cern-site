8

Details

For a vertical track the peak strip sees a ~770 ns flat-topped input, so "10–90 % of max" mostly measures where on the flat top the maximum falls. Binned by that delay, data (X) and the full-ion chain and toy agree within ~10 ns; the no-ion toy is 30–70 ns too fast.

What makes data pulses peak early: the data peak strip passes late charge to its neighbours faster (Y: 0.37 of peak at +480 ns vs the chain's 0.66, ±3 sums agree better); summed charge is mildly front-loaded in X (0.57 vs 0.68 at +600 ns). Drift velocity × gap is consistent: summed signals end together. Scripts: wf_matched.py, wf_sumshape.py.

Details

At 10° / 20° each strip sees only ~110 / ~55 ns of drift, so the waveform is close to the single-electron response and the early-peaking escape of slide 8 does not apply (peak-delay distributions are narrow).

Shape rms against data, 20° / 10° (units of peak, t ≥ −120 ns): chain 0.140 / 0.132; toy full ions 0.183 / 0.171; toy no ions 0.021 / 0.023; toy 110 ns ion 0.029 / 0.035.

A front-loaded ion current (Ψ = (1 − z/g)^p, p ≈ 5–8) fixes the rise (20°: 168 vs 165 ns) but leaves the tail too long. The "10°" sample is the 10.7° ladder point. Scripts: wf_tail20.py, wf_peak_scan.py.

At the real 180 ns shaper, keeping the ions needs an impossibly fast ion

Fit quality of the averaged 10°/20° shapes vs ion transit time (= mobility scaling), four shaper settings.

manual 180 ns (the real setting)

CR-RC² 180 ns

123 ns (ruled out)

At 180 ns the fit only reaches the no-ion level for T ≲ 110 ns: an ion 3–4× faster than Ar⁺. No plausible ion does that.

A 123 ns shaper with an isobutane-like ion (×1.3–1.6) would have fit with no suppression, but the register says 180 ns.

10

Details

S3 drifts ions with AvalancheMC and no ion diffusion (v = K(E)·E along fixed field lines), so scaling K by s rescales the ion current exactly, I(t) → s·I(st). No Garfield rerun is needed for the bracket.

The August "species: 4 % slower" check is invalid: response/meshcell/ion_template_check.py:193 applies Blanc's law to two different ions. In 95/5 the Ar⁺ hands its charge to isobutane within ~ns, and the drifting ion's mobility in argon is ~1.6–1.9 at low field vs Ar⁺'s 1.21 (estimate; Cortez et al., JINST 14 (2019) P04010 for low field, no ~124 Td measurement found).

Open problems and next steps

No mechanism yet for any of the three. Two measurements and one benchmark would narrow it most.

Unexplained

  • Ion signal ≈ 5× suppressed at the true shaper (f_eff ≈ 0.2 vs 0.91)
  • Charge ×1.6 (X) / ×2.1 (Y) low
  • Gain slope ×1.4–1.6 shallow (data slope curved)
  • Early foot before data pulses; −0.2 lobe on sim Y
  • ±1 neighbour delay ×2, Y/X 18 %

Next

  • FEU pulser shot through the test capacitor: the shaper's real response and the absolute ADC/fC scale
  • Look for an ion-suppression mechanism specific to the resistive bulk Micromegas (only sheet screening, ≤ 8 %, sized so far)
  • Garfield literature benchmark: plain bulk MM in Ar/iso 95/5 vs published gain curves (does Magboltz under-slope?)
  • Template-to-template: wft calibrate on sim output vs data
  • Re-quote q_sum on the r06 bundle; redo Y/X without the cut

Not ruled out

  • ρ_s off 2 MΩ/sq (band 1.4–2.6)
  • Gas contamination (no hygrometer on any run)
  • Bench conditions differing from the cfg (HV, temperature)
  • A shared assumption all chain pieces make
