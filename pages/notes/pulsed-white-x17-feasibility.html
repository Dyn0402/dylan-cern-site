Back-n: 298 µs dead time allows one trigger per pulse, and random two-arm triggers fill it (175 per pulse at ES#1, full beam). The throttle answers DREAM, not occupancy.

A beam-synchronous readout of the whole window (~150 of 512 DREAM cells) removes it. ES#1 then meets occupancy 0.93; ES#2, at 0.07, sits on its statistics floor.

GELINA: statistics, then the flash, because its window starts 0.4 µs after it.

4

Details

Full-window readout is an assumption about the existing DREAM hardware, not a tested mode: 150 samples means τ ≈ 2.2 ms, a 5.6 % loss at 25 Hz that is not included (about 3 % on the reach). The Birmingham number is full Geant4 with its room; the others are beam-only re-weightings, so it is not a like-for-like bar.

Measured at n_TOF: the chamber recovers in 0.87 µs, but the DREAM front end rails for 0.5 to more than 25 ms.

Scaling that with neutrons per pulse as a proxy puts unfiltered Back-n at tens of µs or more, longer than its window. It needs a Pb filter and a test.

5

Details

The flash is modelled only as a time cut on each bin. Its size at GELINA and Back-n is not known here; the proxy is neutrons per pulse relative to n_TOF EAR2 (~7e7 above 0.1 MeV): Back-n ES#2 ×0.2, ES#1 ×0.36, GELINA 10 m ×2e-3 (an electron machine makes more γ per neutron, so this one is a lower bound). A Pb filter costs neutrons: 3-5 cm takes ES#2 to about 1.3-1.5e-3 by √flux scaling (ESTIMATE).

What is not settled

Not modelled

• Room, dump and flight-path return: Birmingham’s full Geant4 cost ×1.7 in reach.

• The flash as anything other than a time cut.

• Only the 30 bar × 40 cm cell: the runs do not cover the capsule.

Can be done without any facility

• Pulser-bench test of a 150-sample beam-synchronous DREAM readout.

• Geant4 of the Back-n flash with a Pb filter through the arms.

• The DREAM recovery scaling below the measured 0.5 ms.
