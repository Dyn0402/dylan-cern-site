Every metric: better or worse, and by how much

"Change" is relative for counts and rates, absolute for numbers already in % or mm. The verdict is the direction that helps the physics, not the sign of the change.

1. Capsule focus: does the angle scale point the tracks at the capsule?

Each gated A/C track's x-view line is extended to the beam axis with its tan multiplied by a trial scale s; the median distance by which the lines miss the axis is plotted against s. If the angles are right, the curve is sharpest at s = 1. Before = the is2_v2 re-pass (in-situ bundles, two-track off, cut 0.6). The nominal axis is used here, so the minimum also carries the source's 9 mm offset; the population split below is what separates scale from geometry.

The focus scale split by population (|tan x| 0.1–0.5)

A scale error common to A and C cannot be geometry (distance errors and axis offsets are antisymmetric between the two). Before, both chambers wanted about +10 %. After, the common part is about −2 %, and the A − C difference is under 1 %. Without the angle map, pass 1 had an A − C difference of 4.6 %. The map is fitted to the A–C cosmic lines, and it removes most of that difference.

7. What the angle map and the dead/hot-strip cut changed

Pass 1 was first analysed without two corrections, and this section compares the three versions. The angle map corrects each track's angle as a function of its position and angle. For A and C it is fitted to the A–C cosmic lines, and for D to the B–D lines. The dead/hot-strip cut drops a track when its x fit window lies entirely on dead or hot strips, or when half or more of its y window does. Such a window carries no real charge, but the candidate still won the ranking. These fake tracks were 21 % of D's gated beam tracks and 69 % of its beam-off ones. The cut removes 40 % of D's gated tracks campaign-wide, 8 % of A's (mostly early tracks on A's dead y edge), and 1–2 % of B's and C's.

Run_149 was in the map's training data, so the resolution rows flatter the map. The full table, 106 rows, is ana/track_quality/chains.csv, written by rerun/ntof/improve/compare_chains.py.

Why the pose fit's x χ² rises while the data become more consistent

The x-view χ² of the pose fit has two parts. The shape part compares the x crossing as a function of height with the toy, and this is what the fit learns the pose from. The shape part improves with the map (its best value over all poses goes from 19.5 to 17.9). The crossings part compares A's and C's x crossings with the toy, with 1 mm per chamber. In the toy, A's x crossing lies 2.3–2.7 mm to the right of C's for every one of the 287 poses tried, so no pose can change that difference. In the data, the difference was 0.67 mm without the map and is 0.11 mm with it. Therefore the crossings part rises, and the total rises with it. With the map, A's late and mid windows also agree on the x crossing (−10.87 and −10.96 mm, where they differed by 0.8 mm before). So the data are more consistent, and the 2.3–2.7 mm gap is between the data and the toy. We have not found which side of the comparison carries it: the relative x alignment of A and C, or the toy's geometry.

What is worse, or not settled

  • The capsule focus is less sharp than before (median miss up 2–3 mm at fixed |tan|). Pass 1 keeps about 30 % more late tracks, and the added ones point at the capsule less often. The early (< 12 ms) window gains many tracks with almost no capsule content, so the < 12 ms veto stays.
  • The correlated same-chamber pair fraction is still 6–7× below Geant4 (data/Geant4 0.17 on A, 0.14 on C). The rate fit is fine from 25 ms (χ²/ndf 0.8 on A). The open item is the Geant4 capsule and source model, not the reconstruction.
  • The capsule pose fits are still worse than before (best x χ² 12 → 27 over 10 dof, z 1 → 9 over 5). Part of the x χ² is the A − C crossing gap explained in section 7. The x and z views still prefer different poses.
  • The z band excess seen through D halves with the cut (A–D 0.040 → 0.021). The data's band fraction hardly moves. Instead, the null's band fraction rises, so the earlier D z excess appears to have been inflated by D's fake tracks.
  • B has no angle on pass 1, so the B–D through-goer and B pointing studies lose B.
