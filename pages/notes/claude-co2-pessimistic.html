Pessimistic carbon accounting: Claude vs. reality

A CO2e estimate for the last 30 days of Claude usage on this machine, given as a range — optimistic, best-estimate, and worst-case-on-purpose — stacked against three physical comparisons. Anthropic doesn't publish per-token energy figures, so every number here is an outside estimate, not an audited one.

Claude usage, trailing 30 days (2026-07-12 → 2026-08-11)
0.02 – 16.0 tonnes CO2e
Best estimate: ≈0.26 tonnes (263 kg) — the min and max are what a fully optimistic and fully pessimistic reading of the same 8.19 billion tokens (ccusage local log: 31.4M output, 152.5M cache-write, 8.00B cache-read, 0.17M fresh input, $6,613 billed) produce. The three scenarios differ by 700×, almost entirely on how cheap a cache-read token is assumed to be.
Scope: this laptop only. ccusage reads local session transcripts under ~/.claude/projects/, so this covers Claude Code CLI usage run from this machine — not claude.ai web/mobile usage, direct API usage, or Claude Code run from any other machine. The account-wide total is almost certainly higher.

How each number was built

3 days of 10% CF4 at 10 L/hr

10% of a 10 L/hr mixture is 1 L/hr of pure CF4; over 72 hours that's 72 L. At STP (22.4 L/mol — the denser, more-CO2e end of the room-temp/STP range) that's 3.21 mol, ×88.01 g/mol = 282.9 g CF4. CF4's 100-yr GWP is 7,380 (IPCC AR6) — one of the highest of any gas in routine detector use, and the reason 283 g of it outweighs a transatlantic-length flight leg. 0.2829 kg × 7,380 = 2,088 kg CO2e.

Flight, Paris → Prague

~900 km great-circle. Short-haul European flights run higher CO2/passenger-km than long-haul because take-off and climb dominate a short route — published figures cluster around 150–250 g/pax-km on a direct-emissions basis; this estimate uses 400 g/pax-km, near the top of that band and consistent with including the extra warming effect of contrails and NOx at altitude (commonly quoted radiative-forcing multipliers of ~1.7–2×). 900 km × 0.400 kg/km = 360 kg CO2e.

Van, CERN → CEA Saclay

~560 km one-way by road (Meyrin to the Saclay campus, via Lyon/Paris). Pessimistic diesel cargo-van consumption of 12 L/100km (loaded, mixed motorway/city — well above an efficient ~7–8 L/100km) gives 67.2 L burned. At 2.68 kg CO2/L diesel (tailpipe only; well-to-wheel would run somewhat higher): 67.2 × 2.68 = 180 kg CO2e.

Net picture: the 16-tonne max only gets there by stacking every pessimistic assumption at once, especially costing the 8.0 billion cache-read tokens as if they were fresh computation — that one lever alone accounts for most of the 61× gap to the best estimate. At the best estimate (≈263 kg), this month's Claude usage on this machine is smaller than the Paris–Prague flight, smaller than the CF4 detector-gas leak, and in the same ballpark as one van run to Saclay. The honest takeaway isn't "Claude emits 16 tonnes" — it's that the true number is somewhere on a 700×-wide range because nobody outside Anthropic has the actual per-token energy figure, and cache efficiency is doing most of the work in that uncertainty.
