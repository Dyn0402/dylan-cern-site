Details

The question for the meeting. The n_TOF run cannot see X17 (thermal neutrons only, after the γ-flash dead time; backup B2). Can this detector system search for X17 somewhere else, and what does it need?

Short answer. The Micromegas TPCs track well (best chamber 93 % efficient, 1.2° angles, 176 µm intrinsic) and, in the beam, single tracks agree with Geant4 and image the target. The scintillators hold a muon energy scale that predicts electron spectra to 2–5 %. At LNL (⁷Li(p,e⁺e⁻)⁸Be, the ATOMKI reaction) the Geant4 reach is 17 days to 3σ at the ATOMKI ratio as built and about a day with four large plastics. The neutron facilities are much harder (slide 12).

What is still open. At n_TOF the same-chamber Al e⁺e⁻ pairs come in at ⅛ of the Geant4 prediction while the singles agree; until that is understood, the two-track efficiency for soft, close pairs is not demonstrated (slide 8). X17 pairs are a far easier topology.

Hover the dotted terms and the data points for values, counts and provenance. Main deck: slides 1–13; backup: B1 onward.

Details

Why the Al pairs. After the 1 ms flash veto every neutron is thermal; the trigger fires on the 7.72 MeV Al-capture γ of the capsule vessel (its Compton electrons). About one trigger leg in ten is an e⁺e⁻ made in the capsule. Those pairs are the proof-of-concept sample: ~2 MeV legs that scatter ~9° on the way out (p·β·θ = 0.40 MeV·rad), mostly do not reach the plastic, and land 25° apart in one chamber. That is the hardest two-track case; X17 legs (8–10 MeV typical) scatter 2.3–2.9°, cross the whole stack and go to opposite chambers.

What we can do with the data, for proof of concept: (1) single tracks against Geant4 (rate, source image, angle scale on cosmic muons); (2) the scintillator response and energy scale track by track; (3) the correlated same-chamber pairs (rate, opening angle) against Geant4 pushed through the same reconstruction.

Numbers: x17-status-2026-10-15 slides 3, 9–11 (leg_penetration.py, same_chamber_scatter.py, Athens thermal_accounting). "Pair fires the trigger itself" is the energy-only fold; with the real bar geometry both legs ≥ 2.5 MeV in plastic is 6 % (X17) vs 0 % (Al).

Plan drawing is schematic: arms at 90° without the pinwheel shifts; MM face at 204 mm, SiPM wall ~35 cm, plastic ~41 cm, liquid ~47 cm from the beam.

Details

Correlated pairs. Accidental same-chamber pairs scale with the instantaneous rate, correlated ones do not. Fitting the pair fraction f(t) = a + b·rate(t) over 12–75 ms gives a flat a = 0.15 % (A) / 0.16 % (C) while the rate falls ×3.5: ~2,050 (A) and ~1,660 (C) correlated pairs. Geant4: 75–78 % of reconstructable same-chamber pairs are one photon's e⁺e⁻.

The prediction already includes the opening angle and the close-pair loss: the Geant4 events are digitised into real quiet triggers and run through the production reconstruction (P(2|2) 0.16 / 0.14). 44 % of Geant4 Al pairs are < 20 mm apart in one view. Other efficiency routes bracket data/Geant4 at 0.12–0.52.

Why the vertex test cannot help on Al pairs: two near-parallel ~2 MeV legs, each bent ~9°, cross ~75 mm from where they were born, so even perfect tracks pass 2 % of the time (chance 3 %).

Source: x17-status-2026-10-15 slides 4–11; same_chamber_compare.py, /media/dylan/data/x17/sept26_prelim/same_chamber/cmp_*.csv.

The stack measures energy: calibrated on muons alone, the plastics predict electron spectra to 2–5 %

self-triggered single tracks at n_TOF · spectra: both bars, all times · scales: per bar, ≥ 40 ms · Geant4 with the measured trigger and resolution

Linear from the muon's 3.4 MeV to stopping electrons at 3–8 MeV; stable to 0.5–0.8 % run to run over the production period.

Bar C1 reads 1.15: a pure gain offset of unknown cause. Plastic PMTs lose 3–8 % gain 12–25 ms after the flash (rate-proof bases or a pulser next time).

At n_TOF the 2 cm plastic stops only the soft leg (a range telescope). With 4–5 cm plates the full stack is a calorimeter: the LNL E_sum and pair-mass fits rest on it. Resolution: to be measured on a prototype plate.

MM charge: a gain monitor (19–39 % per 40 mm cell), not a dE/dx or two-particle flag (FWHM/MPV 0.95).

Details

The test (ntof_calorimetry/wall_plastic.py, note scint-calorimetry): calibrate the SiPM wall and each plastic bar on cosmic muons only (beam-off runs on the n_TOF clock, path from the MM track), then predict the beam-electron spectra with Geant4 of the full stack (total prompt deposit in the matched bar, smeared with the measured σ/MPV, the arm's trigger applied in MeV). Geant4 is drawn at scale 1.

Scales (Geant4 × s matches the data mean over 3–8 MeV, bootstrap errors): plastic A1/A2/C2 1.05/1.03/1.02, C1 1.15; wall A/C 0.95/0.96. Most of these electrons stop in the plastic (below ~6 MeV), so their deposit is their energy. A 25 mm bar (the run-config text) would put every scale at ×1.27 — excluded, so the 20 mm in Geant4 is right.

Reconciling with the 8 Oct "calorimetry closed". That closure was about what the n_TOF stack can do for X17 pairs: with 20 mm of plastic and ~1.2 MeV lost upstream, the soft-leg energy adds only 5–8 % to the significance, and the liquids are threshold-limited. The 14 Oct result above is about the scintillators: their energy scale works. For a future run, 4–5 cm plates contain the leptons and the LNL study's E_sum / m_ee fit assumes exactly this n_TOF-anchored response (as built it loses the mass fit, backup B4).

Why it can work where n_TOF could not

· DC beam, no γ flash: the DAQ is 91–98 % live up to 4 µA.

· ~900 X17 made per day at 1 µA, at the exact ATOMKI kinematics.

Details

The curve (lnl/plastic_size.py, an analytic stand-in for a Geant4 size scan): straight leptons from a 2 mm beam spot through the surveyed MM active area, Highland kicks in the MM and the SiPM wall, a leg must cross a read-out SiPM bar and the plastic face above an energy threshold. Two free numbers are fitted to the Geant4 pair tag as built and with 75×75 cm plastics. Days to 3σ scale as B/S² in 125–155°, anchored to the Geant4 big-plastics value; E_sum, MM 15° and TOF efficiencies are held at their Geant4 values, so it is about geometry only. Where the plates would collide with their neighbours they are pushed back (beyond ~75 cm).

Why it flattens: past ~65–75 cm the plate covers the whole cone of leptons that crossed an MM (92 % of X17 leptons at 75×75); the SiPM wall (83 %) is then the cap. Removing the wall (orange) leaves a plastic-only leg and gains a little more.

Details

n_TOF: mpgd26 / ntof_athens_26 (the Athens deck): the γ flash saturates DREAM (detector recovers in 2 µs, DREAM in 5 ms); every volt of gain costs ms of beam; the run moved to thermals, where (n,p) dominates and the ³He self-shields: 4.4 → 0.07 X17/day in 0.01–0.1 eV, ≈100 → ≈2 X17 made over the campaign's 436,373 pulses. Below ~2 eV only s-wave channels are open (M1, and E0 with no real photon; CLAUDE.md / ipc_born.py).

ILL (ill/FEASIBILITY_SIM.md §13): He flight tube + Be 0.25 mm (or 25 µm mylar) window cut the trigger rate 249 → 8–23 Hz and halve the MM occupancy; with a ceiling cosmic panel and E_sum > 14 MeV the reach is 6.5×10⁻³ at 1.9×10¹⁰ n/s and 5.3×10⁻³ at 6×10¹⁰ with a Ø4 cm spot (×3.2 from ⁸Be). Walls: MM occupancy + DREAM (3.4×10⁻³ if both cut ×10), then IPC statistics (2.1×10⁻³ at 1.2×10¹¹, ×1.3). Spin selection with polarised n + ³He is the extra lever (POLARISED.md). ³He: bare mylar loses ~22 L per cycle → the PET + Al laminate skin (he4_bag §6).

Sketch: G1 (HANDOFF_SIM §4): 1 bar, 300 mm of gas, R 40 mm, entrance window at y_w = −21.6 mm so the median capture sits at the MM centre; 99.5 % of the H113 beam stops inside; downstream cap at +278 mm (1.2 % of vertices beyond the +180 mm MM edge). The ⁴He "pre-chamber" is the flight tube from the aperture to the cell window: ⁴He captures no neutrons and scatters ~13× less than air; ~90 % of the air ¹⁴N(n,γ) is on that 300 mm path. A balloon around the whole cell is not worth it (⁴He permeates into the cell and the MM gas). Shading = capture density along the cell (schematic exponential with the G1 median).

NFS (ganil_nfs/README.md §7, §9): d+Be peaks at 17 MeV, measured only above 1.5 MeV; options: lower deuteron energy, D(d,n)³He monoenergetic (~2.45 MeV, may never have been built), ⁷Li(p,n) near threshold. The X17 shoulder slides to 99° at 2 MeV and 66° at 14 MeV, into the IPC-rich region.
