Details

L4 started every muon on a 3 × 3 m plane 1.5 m above the target: a muon more than ~45° off the zenith never reaches the set-up's centre. L15 (new option --cosmic-disk 900) starts each muon on a 90 cm disk normal to its own direction: every zenith angle up to 89°, cos²θ intensity, 1 cm⁻² min⁻¹ through a horizontal surface. Same Cu-hemisphere geometry, all four arms, cut to pairs. The vertical pair's coincidences pile up at 125–160°, the peak the MEG II members' 2026 study found in ATOMKI-type spectrometers (arXiv:2609.18383).

Details

Why horizontal for the beam days: with working vetoes the two opposite pairs are equal (±10 %); if the vetoes disappoint, the horizontal pair sees ~10× fewer cosmics. Why vertical before the beam: cosmics need no beam, and the vertical pair records them at full strength, including the 140° peak MEG II pointed at; a few nights measure the arm-to-arm time of flight with ~10⁵ crossing muons per day. Why 441 keV for one day: ~500 M1 pairs above 90° per day in the opposite pair, a 5 % check of the absolute IPC rate and angular shape against Geant4 with no anomaly expected. Why adjacent at the end: the external conversions in the Cu wall are the least-known background; adjacent arms see them at 25–90° at several thousand per day.
