Details

Beam curve. The EAR2 evaluated flux the Geant4 gun samples (fluxEAR2-Ph3_in_different_units.root, n/pulse per bin) times a thin 1/v absorber, at 19.5 m. The Al nose is thin for neutrons, so this is the time profile of the beam's own capture γ. Within the 1–75 ms acquisition window: 87% before 12 ms.

Capsule-pointing tracks. Per ToF bin, the fraction of tracks whose x line passes within 15 mm of the late-time band crossing (A −10.2, C −10.7 mm in global x), minus the same with the angles shuffled between tracks (the chance rate, ~10–12 %), times the tracks per ms. This is a pointing measure, not a shape fit.

What the late capsule tracks are. They cannot be direct beam neutrons: the flux has essentially nothing after 28 ms, and half of the capsule-pointing tracks come after 30 ms with a ~40 ms decay. They are delayed (thermalised, room-return) neutrons captured in the capsule's aluminium, the same population the late-trigger clock found (T½ ≈ 24 ms; ntof_cosmics/HANDOFF_TRACKING_2026-10-06.md §10). Their source height is the same as the beam's would be (slide 6), so they are captured at the same end of the capsule.

Details

Purity. At 2–10 ms only 13% (A) / 13% (C) of tracks point at the capsule, against 46% / 41% after 20 ms. The arm's own SiPM wall fired for 51% / 74% of the early tracks and 93% / 99% of the late ones. The late data are at least as pure as Geant4's capsule-only sample through the reconstruction (31% / 34%), so the late denominator is not inflated by non-capsule tracks. That rules out "other sources in the data" as the factor-8 explanation for t ≥ 12 ms.

Pair fraction. It is 1–14 % before 10 ms and 0.2–0.4 % after; the 12–75 ms fit f = a + b·rate gives the correlated a. Early pairs are mostly pile-up and flash junk; the correlated pairs are there too, in proportion: with a constant a they would be ~0.15 % of the ~465 k early A events, about 700 pairs, against 15–30 k early pairs in total. So "junk" means signal-to-background of a few percent, not "no signal".

Can the early window be recovered? Only with a cleaner early trigger and a faster DAQ, which is now moot for this dataset. For the analysis the cut costs about a quarter of the correlated pairs; a purity cut (lines through the capsule) applied before pairing could win some of it back. The 2026-10-13 vertex artefact came from mixing on early events, so any early-time pair work needs its own early-time mixing pool.

Scattering on the way out, not the reconstruction, breaks the Al pair vertex

Geant4 truth, 13,545 conversion leptons from the 10⁹-neutron nose campaign · p·β·θ = 0.40 MeV·rad at every energy

Geant4 neutrons_thermal_trig_2cm_nose (nose-first capsule, centred beam), same_arm_pairs.py truth per lepton

4

Details

What is measured. For each conversion lepton that crosses ≥ 20 mm of a drift gap: the line through its first gap step along its gap direction, and that line's distance of closest approach to the true conversion point. The deflection is the angle between the vertex→gap chord and the gap direction. No reconstruction is involved.

The law. The median p·β·θ is 0.40 MeV·rad in every bin from 0.5 to 6 MeV (0.31 at 6–9 MeV, n = 92), which is multiple scattering: θ ∝ 1/(pβ). The path is the capsule wall (0.6 mm Al barrel, up to 5.5 mm at the nose, 0.9 mm CFRP), then about 23 cm of air and the drift cathode. The median conversion lepton has 2.1 MeV at the gap, so it arrives 9° off its birth direction.

X17 legs (8–10 MeV) are scaled with the same law: 2.3–2.9°, miss 9–11 mm, comparable to the angular resolution (~7 mm). 90%–82% of X17 legs pass within 20 mm of their vertex, against 31% of Al legs.

Details

Why near-parallel lines are the problem. The intersection of two lines at opening angle α moves by about δ / sin α along the bisector when one line is displaced by δ. With δ ≈ 33 mm and α ≈ 25°, the midpoint lands ~78 mm away; Geant4 says 75 mm (median). Small angles are worst (0–15°: 1.6 %), and no bin passes more than 3 %.

The X17 case is different in all three factors. Its legs carry 4–5× the momentum, so the miss per leg is ~9 mm; they go to opposite chambers, so the vertex test is "does each line pass the axis" rather than "where do two near-parallel lines cross"; and the 470 mm A–C lever arm makes the line itself well defined. The 67–82 % assumes the same wall and air as the Al pairs (X17 is born in the gas and crosses the same wall) and ignores resolution, which adds ~7 mm in quadrature.

So the 2026-10-12 "vertex excess" could never have been the Al pairs: a test that real pairs pass 2 % of the time and chance passes 2–3 % of the time cannot show a ×2 excess of them. The late-only result (A ×1.00) is what Geant4 predicts.

Details

Transverse. From campaign_imaging (per-run band crossings, post-access runs): A −8.3, C −10.3 (global x; their half-difference is the A–C alignment), D −3.5, B −4.1 (global z, B from far fewer tracks). capsule_tof tilt reproduces x on is2_v2 cf (A −10.2, C −10.7). The sign convention is the one of GEOMETRY_COORDINATE_CONVENTION.md: A +z, B −x, C −z, D +x. So the source is displaced toward B (−x) and slightly toward C (−z).

What the offset means. The imaging measures where captures happen relative to the chambers, not where the beam is. The beam is narrow at thermal energies (50 % within r = 2.5 mm, 90 % within 13 mm; Lambda2D). If it is centred on the chamber frame and the capsule sits 10 mm off it, only ~half the beam meets the capsule, the nose core gets ~5 %, and ~11 % grazes the barrel wall, where a ray can run 40 mm through aluminium. If beam and capsule are offset together, the nose is hit as in Geant4. The source height can tell these apart, but most capsule-pointing tracks are delayed neutrons rather than beam (slide 2), so it is not yet settled. A pose fit is handed off (HANDOFF_CAPSULE_POSE_FIT.md).

Height and the y sign. The y-strip direction was fixed in software on 2026-09-07 (build_tracks.IN_PLANE_SIGN_Y = −1) by requiring corr(y, tan y) > 0. That test cannot tell "positions flipped" from "tans flipped": both make the correlation positive, and they differ by exactly a mirror of the vertical axis. For x the pinwheel offset decided it; y has no such offset. The July hits-based analysis, which did not flip positions, put the source "~3 cm low, the bottom of the capsule". The 2026-09-14 capsule-height handoff then concluded that the CAD height was wrong by 30 mm. That conclusion and this flip are the same 60 mm.

A global y mirror leaves every angle between tracks unchanged (opening angles, X17 included). It changes absolute heights: the source height, target_y_mm, the pair-vertex y window (CAPSULE_V in same_chamber_physics), the 32.2 mm shift used to compare with Geant4, and every v-map of the scintillators.

Details

Shear. Both chambers, both chains (is2_v2 cf and production), show the source x moving from about −14 mm at the lowest reconstructed heights to −9.3 mm at +40…+60. The same estimator on Geant4's untilted capsule is flat within ~1 mm, so it is not built in by the estimator. The slope is ≈ 0.05 (≈ 3°) and, since height is smeared by the resolution, the true slope is at least that. Two readings: the capsule is tilted in the x–y plane (the B side), or both chambers are rotated in their planes by the same angle; A–C cosmics are blind to the second. With y corrected (next paragraph), the top of the source is further toward −x (B) than the bottom, the opposite sense to a "bottom furthest out" tilt.

Liquid B. The horizontal liquids (A, D) answer 10–20× more on their PMT half. B, vertical with its PMT on top per the 17–18 July survey, rises steadily toward reconstructed −y, by ×2. With y flipped that is toward its PMT, like A and D. C has too few counts to say. This is the open "why don't the vertical liquids answer near their PMT" question of the scint-stack note, and a y flip would answer it.

Settled by the wiring (Dylan, 2026-10-13): low FEU channels are at the bottom of each chamber. mx17_m1_map.csv puts y = 0.78 mm × channel, and each chamber's local y is global +y, so the raw strip coordinate already points up. build_tracks.IN_PLANE_SIGN_Y = −1 then mirrors it. The correct fix in 2026-09 was to flip tan y, not the position. Every stored height is mirrored: the source is at −31 (A) / −35 (C) mm, at the nose. The stage-3 fix is pending (Dylan's call); a pose fit is handed off in HANDOFF_CAPSULE_POSE_FIT.md.

One compact source on the capsule nose, ~10 mm off the frame centre, at every ToF

Details

Measured. The tracks image one compact source at x -10.4, z ≈ −3.6 mm in the chamber frame, at the nose's height (-31.3 A / -34.6 C). Its x is the same from 2 to 75 ms (the pointing-excess peak, background-robust where the band crossing is not), in every run, and before and after the 27 July access. A and C agree although their u axes point opposite ways, so only a common shift of the pair against the beam could fake it.

What it does not say. The source is where the illuminating neutrons meet Al, not the beam. A compact source at the nose needs a narrow flux on the nose (within ~8 mm, capsule within ±5 mm of its drawn height); grazing lights the 0.6 mm barrel and lifts the source 10–20 mm. A Lambda2D beam centred on the frame reaches x ≈ −10 only with the capsule 13–18 mm off, a flux ×10 wider, or the tail alone, and then puts the source 14–25 mm too high (capsule that much lower than drawn; 1–15 % of the beam on the ³He). The direct-beam window shows no source near x = 0 (≤ 10–34 % at 4–6 ms, ≤ 5 % at 6–12 ms), but the share of direct-beam captures there is unknown: the late, after-the-flux population images the same spot.

Two readings. (1) The flux sits on the capsule, ~10 mm toward B from the frame centre: the beam is not where the frame says, or the frame is not where it was aligned (≥ 85% of the beam then enters the ³He). (2) The beam is centred as aligned and the capsule is far off it and lower than drawn (³He yield 1–15 %). Needs a survey or photo of capsule vs beam, the tip height, and the beam spot at the station. Neither explains the 1 : 8 alone. Report: <out>/capsule_pose/report.html.

Details

The direct route (same_chamber_compare.expectation, "digitised Geant4 through the reconstruction"): r₂ = Geant4 events with ≥ 2 leptons in the gap per event with ≥ 1, times P(2 tracks | 2 leptons) / P(≥ 1 | 1) measured on g4_digi (A 0.54, C 0.45 for one lepton). Because the digitised events carry Geant4's own opening angles and separations, the close-pair loss and the opening-angle distribution are in the prediction, as they were in that reconstruction pass. Data/Geant4 = 0.13 (A), 0.12 (C).

The bracket routes use the measured per-view two-track efficiency of is2_v2 cf (two_track_efficiency_axis.csv) on Geant4's true separations; they give 0.12–0.52.

Next iteration. Getting below 20 mm (T1's joint fit, off in this pass) would add up to ~45 % more pairs in both data and prediction. It raises the statistics; it does not by itself change the ratio. Re-digitising with is2_v2 is the step that fixes the prediction's reconstruction, and it should be done before the next comparison.

The difference comes mostly from the 0–10° and 40–50° bins, so it is a preference, not a separation; the Geant4 histogram has only ~60 weighted pairs per arm and its own error is in the χ². C does not discriminate (its Geant4 χ² is the worse one). Medians: data A 25°, Geant4 26°. A global y flip would not move any of this: opening angles are mirror-invariant.

What this does not rule out, and what to do next

not ruled out

  • The 1 : 8 rate is still unexplained. The late data are as capsule-pure as Geant4, the expectation already pays for opening angle and close-pair loss, and the transverse offset costs percent.
  • The x-vs-height step (~4 mm) is a ~3.6σ hint of a second, higher source; a 10× Geant4 digitised sample would settle it.
  • Late capsule captures are delayed neutrons, not the simulated beam. If they land elsewhere on the capsule than beam neutrons do, the Al thickness around them, and so the conversion yield per trigger, differs from Geant4.
  • A busy-event two-track loss in data that the quiet digitisation lacks is still open.
