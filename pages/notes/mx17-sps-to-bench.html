Details

Question (Dylan, 2026-10-11): the SPS beam gas attached electrons (O₂, ~150–230 ppm; the bench gas is only moist). Does that contaminate the model parameters measured at SPS so that they no longer apply where there is no attachment?

Answer: almost entirely no. Every SPS constant the bench model takes is measured either at the leading edge (before any loss acts) or through a relation in which the arrival current, attachment included, cancels. An injection test that runs the SPS measurement code itself on events with known constants, with the run_71 loss on and off, moves them by ≤ 6%. The exception is the prompt/delayed split of the old decomposition (b1 -27%, τ), which the physical model no longer uses.

What does not transfer is what never would have: the gas (v, drift diffusion, the loss itself), and setup-specific calibrations. The bigger transfer issue turns out to be bench drift diffusion, which is degenerate with D_rc and σ0 on the bench (slide 6).

Why attachment mostly cannot reach them: it lives in the drive every strip shares

Head-on, every strip sees the same arrival current through its own lateral filter

Details

For a track normal to the view, every depth lands at the same lateral position. Each strip's signal is then the same drive C(t) (arrival current × electronics) passed through that strip's lateral transfer n_d. Attachment changes C(t) and nothing else, so any relation built from the strips' own signals — the cross-relation, or the RC fit with the measured strip sum as the source — is blind to it.

The cancellation is exact only if the lateral transfer is the same for every depth. Drift diffusion breaks that: charge from deeper lands wider. With a depth-dependent footprint the fit sees a mixture whose depth weights attachment changes. The size of that leak is what the injection test measures, and it is small because the beam gas diffuses little (D_T 160–240 µm/√cm, σ ≤ 0.4 mm at the cathode).

Details

rc_drift.py is rc_diffusion.fit with drift diffusion written in: the stack's source sample at time t is taken to have arrived u = t − t_a after the first charge, and lands with width² = σ0² + D_T²·v·u + 2·D_rc·s. D_T and v are Magboltz at the fitted run_71 composition; t_a is fitted. On the beam t_a runs to its upper bound (the data want even less depth growth than Magboltz gives), so the Magboltz arm is an upper bound on the diffusion correction.

The toy says the run_71 loss lowers the fitted D_rc by a few per cent; the corrected value divides that out.

The bench's D_rc depends on the drift diffusion assumed; det4 sits 1.5–2.7× its beam value

Details

The structural results are the main export: they hold for every chamber and do not depend on any number measured in the beam gas. The numbers are det4's; the bench measures the other four chambers' σ0 and D_rc itself, with the degeneracy of the bench slide.

The 176 µm intrinsic resolution is a beam-condition performance number. The loss removes on average ~18 % of the charge at 243 V/cm, so a charge-statistics-limited resolution would be ≲ 10 % better without it; it is quoted as measured, in the H4 gas.

The det4 beam calibration bundles (share_lp, beam-pinned) carry the old copy kernel and the H4 v and Dp; they are not used on the bench.

What this does not rule out, and what is next

  • det4 bench vs beam D_rc (1.5–2.7×). Not attachment, not drift diffusion within its range. Resistive layer conditions, bench selection, or a model term both fits lack.
  • The bench drift diffusion. v(E) says ~1 % water (D_T ≈ 270); the widths look dry (≈ 480). It sets how well the bench can measure σ0 and D_rc at all.
  • Toy realism below 243 V/cm. The toy's widths grow with falling field, the data's shrink. Shifts quoted from 243 V/cm only.
  • Three-body O₂ scale. Irrelevant here: the test uses the measured η·v, not the ppm.
