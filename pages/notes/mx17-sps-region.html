Details

The whole-beam-spot SPS numbers (rerun/sps_audit) average over everything det4 had at H4: dead amplification stripes, live bands of different gain (lead-strip charge ×1.6 between them) and the three pitch zones of the uRWELL telescope's back plane. On det4 X those zones land on different live bands, so the free 3-zone fit that should separate det4 from the telescope mixes the two and fails (χ²/1 = 7–88). This note picks one region where det4 works in both views and measures everything there.

Code: rerun/sps_region/ (region_analysis.py, eff_job.py, trigger_ref.py, this deck make_deck.py). Inputs: the final SPS pass telescope tables (x17rerun/sps_audit/tel/F*, code 3ac0e79), the footprint tables, the Paper I timing strip sums (code 48244778), and a new run-63 efficiency table with every telescope track (x17rerun/sps_region/eff/).

Details

How the map is made. Every single-cluster four-view uRWELL track (eff_job.py, all 147,279 tracks in the run-63 flat700 plateau) is projected to det4 with the same telescope→det4 affine the resolution uses. A track counts as seen if det4 has a local maximum ≥ 50 ADC (5 × noise) within ±2 mm of the prediction in X and in Y. The vertical dark bands are det4's dead amplification stripes (fixed, measured on the bench too). At H4 the chamber was rolled 90°, so in the hall they ran horizontally.

Why R = X 150–160, Y 222–246 mm:

  1. X: the live band 148–162 mm is the widest with the highest gain (lead-strip charge ~330 ADC against ~210 in the 201–214 band). R keeps ≥ 2 mm from both stripe edges.
  2. Y ≥ 222 mm puts both telescope coordinates in the back plane's 1.0 mm pitch zone (edge at Y ≈ 220 mm), so the telescope pointing is the same for every track in R.
  3. Clear of the oscillating Y channels: ch 372–376 (154.6–158.7 mm) and ch 510 (250.4 mm). Y ends at 246 mm.
  4. In the densest part of the spot: 6,311 telescope tracks with a det4 record.

R2 (X 202–213 mm, same Y) is the same recipe in the second band. It is used only as a cross-check.

Details

This is why the whole-spot X resolution could not be decomposed: the telescope's back-plane zone is set by the track's y on the uRWELL, which at H4 maps onto det4 X. The 1.0 mm zone covers the high-gain band 148–162 mm, the 1.5 mm zone the lower-gain band 201–214 mm and the 0.5 mm zone only the narrow band at 232 mm. A 3-zone fit of det4 X therefore fits gain differences as if they were telescope pitch: χ²/1 = 7–88 in rerun/sps_audit. Inside one region the zone is fixed and the gain is uniform, so the problem goes away. The telescope term then has to come from somewhere else (next slides).

Gain: median charge of the X lead strip for tracks det4 reads within ±1.5 mm of the telescope. It falls from ~400 to ~280 ADC across the 148–162 band and is ~210 ADC in the 201–214 band.

Details

Model (as sps_audit/analyze.py): σ²res = σ²det4 + (z/zback)²(f·pitch)² + (1 − z/zback)²(1 mm/√12)², with z = 1120 mm (det4) and zback = 1370 mm. Inside one det4 X band, det4 Y crosses the 1.5 and 1.0 mm zones of the back plane's x coordinate. The 0.5 mm zone (Y 164–173) has only 50–270 tracks there, at the edge of the beam and of the band, so it is not used. Two zones and two unknowns: the fit is exact (χ² = 0), and its errors come from the residual widths. It assumes det4 Y is uniform along Y within the band. The gain profile along Y is flat there (±10 %).

f is a property of the telescope, so one value, the mean of the six fits, is used for every estimator: f = 0.266, systematic range 0.254–0.284. The whole-spot fits of sps_audit gave 0.25–0.34; their upper end comes from the failed X fits (previous slide). The X view's telescope coordinate is the back plane's y, assumed to have the same f. The check on the next slide supports this: selecting 2-strip telescope clusters removes the same ~200 µm (in quadrature) from both views.

Details

Two ways to read the telescope out of the residual.

  1. Model-free upper bound. When the back-plane cluster of the coordinate in question has 2 strips, its position is interpolated and the telescope pointing is much better than for a 1-strip (binary) cluster. The residual of that subsample is det4 ⊕ (a smaller) telescope, so it bounds det4 from above: X ≤ 176 ± 6 µm (894 tracks), and ≤ 153 ± 6 µm (264) when the front cluster is 2-strip too. Y ≤ 145 ± 6 µm (731). (The front x plane has too few 2-strip clusters for a Y b2f2 bound.)
  2. Subtracted. σdet4 = √(σ²res − P²) on all tracks, P = 224 µm (zone 1.0, f = 0.266). The f range gives the systematic (table, last column).

Consistency: the f model predicts the telescope pointing of the 2-strip subsample, Pb2 = √(P² − (σ²all − σ²b2)). It comes out positive and similar in both views: X 109 µm, Y 82 µm. So the X/Y difference belongs to det4, not to the telescope.

Details

R2 is X 202–213 mm, the same Y range, in the second live band: lead-strip charge ~210 ADC against ~330, and its X coordinate sits in the telescope's 1.5 mm zone (pointing ~0.33 mm, so its X subtraction is larger and less certain). Its det4 values are higher in both views. The resolution follows the local gain, so the whole-spot numbers are averages over regions of different quality.

run 66: the two highest-gain resist plateaus (755, 780 V), 32 samples (the drift column is cut), 243 V/cm. run 53: Ar/CO2/iso at a lower gain (lead charge ~110 ADC), no drift velocity in the registry, so the wft fit and p_mid are not meaningful there; the charge centroid is shown. Its X centroid is bad, as it is in run 63; its Y centroid agrees with run 63's.

Details

Denominator: every telescope track with exactly one cluster in each of the four uRWELL plane-views, inside the run-63 flat700 plateau (±20 s from its edges), predicted in R by the run-63 telescope→det4 affine. Numerator: det4 has a local maximum of the per-strip peak amplitude ≥ 50 ADC within ±2 mm of the prediction, in X and in Y (oscillating and coupled channels excluded, the final SPS rules). New table: eff_job.py → x17rerun/sps_region/eff/ (147,279 tracks).

It is a lower bound on det4's efficiency. The single-cluster telescope track is another particle, or out of time, in part of the events: in sps_audit 7–13 % of single-cluster tracks have front and back planes more than 3 mm apart. det4 has some signal in both views in 97.8 % of these events, and the efficiency grows with the matching window (86 / 90 / 91 % at ±1 / 2 / 3 mm), as expected if the missing part is telescope tails and pile-up rather than det4 inefficiency.

Uniformity: the eight cells range 88–92 %. The X half 155–160 mm is ~3 points lower. The gain also falls there towards the stripe edge at 162.5 mm (slide 3).

Details

Quantities per event (sps_cluster via the pass tables): number of strips ≥ 50 ADC within ±4 samples of the lead strip's peak (censored strips bridged); lead fraction = lead peak ÷ sum of the cluster's peaks; neighbour ratio = each ±1 neighbour's peak ÷ the lead's (median over both sides). run 63: telescope-matched tracks in R (both views within ±1.5 mm), 5,247 events. run 71 RAW (raw700, 243 V/cm, no zero suppression): det4's own cluster position selects R, since the footprint table has no telescope (117 of 2,936 events).

Without zero suppression (run 71) the footprint is threshold-limited only by the 50 ADC cut, so it is the closest to the physical one. On ZS rows a suppressed neighbour reads 0, which raises the lead fraction and changes the neighbour ratio; on run 63 the X neighbour ratio is 0.47 against Y 0.36, mostly the ZS threshold (sps_audit §5). The kernel itself (the ±1/±2 sharing in the reconstruction) is the pass calibration's.

In R the RAW sample is small (117 events), so its means carry ~±0.1 strips.

Details

What is measured. det4's X and Y strips read the same avalanches, so tX − tY cancels the particle time, the primary-cluster positions and the trigger. Its spread /√2 is the per-view part of the time resolution: the readout and the estimator. On the bench the same quantity is 22–28 ns on the golden rows, and the event time there is 40–46 ns because a common part of 35–40 ns (primary clusters near the mesh) adds to it (rerun/paper1_timing).

Estimators (paper1_timing/estimators.py): edge = a step convolved with the g_det4 electronics template, fitted to the rising edge up to 25 % of the strip sum's maximum; CF 20 % = where the strip sum first crosses 20 % of its maximum, linearly interpolated. Strip sums: paper1_timing/sps_edge.py (6000 events per row, ~17 s of each plateau; both views clustered, charge ≥ 150 ADC). R is selected on det4's own cluster positions; there is no telescope in these tables.

Trigger. In the uRWELL telescope's hits (banco's combined_hits, run 63, nine files, interpolated hit times), the largest hit per event has s68 = 18.5 ns (17–20 ns per file) against the trigger. A trigger uniform in the 60 ns clock period alone would give ~20 ns, so the H4 trigger plus the DREAM time base contribute at most ~19 ns (trigger_ref.py). The (X+Y)/2 spread against the trigger in R (last column, 140–250 ns) is much larger than either the trigger or the per-view part. Most likely it is untagged pile-up in the 3.84 µs RAW window (only 25 % of run-71 telescope tracks pass the cross-view fiducial in sps_audit), which X − Y does not see. It is not quoted as det4's event time.

What this does not rule out

• The subtracted resolutions depend on the telescope f (0.25–0.28 measured in-band); the upper bounds do not.

• X is worse than Y with every estimator; why is open (X ±1 asymmetry is a candidate, not shown).

• Event time (the common part) is not measured at SPS; only the per-view part is.

• One patch, one gas (Ar/CF4/iso 88/10/2): not n_TOF's gas or gain, and not the whole chamber.

Details

Decision for the papers: quote these numbers as "det4 in a fully working region at SPS", with the upper bounds, or keep the whole-spot numbers (Y 190 ± 10 µm, X model-dependent). The region numbers are cleaner: one telescope zone, one gain, no dead strips. They are also smaller, because the whole spot averages in lower-gain bands. Say which in the text.
