Raw tan is w / v. Putting the 42.6 prior in place of the speed the kernel was fitted with shrinks every raw tan by v_true/42.6, so production needs k ≈ 1.16 (A) and 1.60 (C) just to undo it.

6

Details

This is the chain that closed 7 Oct: (1) cosmics read the same at A's wall as on the A–C line, so the wall survey and the A–C truth are consistent; (2) A–C muons inside beam runs read the cosmic scale within 2–5 % with 34 % less gain and full beam-on noise, so the beam environment is not the cause; (3) Geant4 with an ideal reconstruction gives the beam-electron wall reading (0.59 of the gap angle, energy-dependent).

Consequence: neither the wall nor the capsule band is beam angle truth without a forward model. The in-beam muons are. Source: §10c–10g, §13.

Details

Details

Found while writing this note (repass_readiness.py beam). The smoke test compared condor with the local is2 reco (identical); nobody had compared is2's yield with production's. The seeder test of §10a (+45/52 % confirmed) used the production bundle.

Why it bites. wft.reco._candidate_score: plausible = 250 ≤ q_uend ≤ 1100 ns and |fit.tan_theta| < 0.6, where tan_theta is the raw tan of the bundle. An implausible fit loses the candidate ranking (key = (plausible, Δχ²)) and fails the x/y pairing gate. Raw tan ∝ 1/v, so the cut's true-angle meaning is 0.6 × k.

Are the lost tracks real? Partly. Their arm-level scintillator coincidence (coinc_this_arm) is 34 % against 40–64 % for gated 0.3–0.6 tracks, and 26–40 % have t0 > 300 ns (the late class with unreliable geometry, T3). Large-angle tracks also miss the wall more often, so the wall test is partly blind to them. Candidates passing everything but the cut, raw 0.6 / 0.8 / 1.0 / 1.2: C is2 10,159 / 12,121 / 14,077 / 14,801.

Options. (a) keep 0.6 raw ≈ 0.58 true and record it as the acceptance (the cosmic truth covers only |tan| < 0.6); (b) make TAN_MAX a true-angle cut carried by the bundle (e.g. 0.9–1.0) and validate 0.6–1.0 with digitised Geant4 muons/electrons at those angles plus scintillator confirmation, then rerun the smoke sub-run. Other raw-tan constants: TAN_MIN_SLOPE 0.08 (slope_reliable) and FLOOR_TAN 0.018 shift by the same factor, which matters less.

Details

Reading the plot. Each row is a truth, plotted as the factor it would apply to is2's delivered tans. The cosmic A–C line and the in-beam muons are both muons; the capsule focus is beam electrons (few MeV, mostly Compton electrons from capture gammas). The Geant4 row is the focus estimator run on simulated electrons through the same reco: it reads a correct scale as 1.00 ± 0.03.

Equivalent statement. s = 1.12 is the same as the tracks converging 28 mm beyond the beam axis as seen from each chamber. Because that is on opposite sides for A and C, no single displaced source does it (that is the antisymmetric dx, which was fitted out); it would take a vertex distribution that is "far" from both chambers at once, or an electron angle response ~12 % shallower than the muons'.

History of the sign. The band residual (§13) already had A +3.6 %, C +10 % on the same side, but the band is source-width biased. The focus is not (in the sim), so the residual is now a measurement, not an artefact of the estimator. Where to go next: two cheap checks decide whether the 12 % is real

In order. 1 and 2 test the two inferences each side rests on; 3 and 4 only if both hold.

< 1 h, stage 3

~1 day

days

Details

Why this order. Each side of the disagreement rests on one inferred step. The muon side: the norm was measured with the production bundle and carried to is2 assuming is2 raw = production raw × a constant, which the cosmic closure supports only to its own precision. The capsule side: "the focus is unbiased" was shown in Geant4 with the simulated source and without the offset the data turned out to have. Checks 1 and 2 remove those two steps, for an hour and a few hours.

If both hold, the difference is physics of the data, not of the estimators: either the vertex distribution (3) or electrons responding differently from muons in the real chamber, which only an electron truth (4) can show. Until then, option 5 keeps downstream work moving without choosing.

What not to do. Do not refit k on the capsule: that is the k_arm logic production used (A 1.27), and it is what the in-situ calibration replaced. A wider stage-2 pass is also off the table (§21: steep tracks are background).

Which scale is right on A and C

Muons and the capsule disagree by ~12 % on both arms; neither has been shown wrong.

Electrons ≠ muons in the real chamber

The digitiser reproduces muon-like response for electrons in the model, not in the chamber.

The source model

The data image is tighter than the simulated one and sits 9.5 mm off axis.

Angles above |tan| 0.6

No truth there; data at 0.5–0.6 already points poorly.

B and D

No in-situ truth; null angles.
