The short answer

Recommended for the first run (B)

  • Split each scintillator signal with CAEN N979 fast amplifiers: two full-amplitude copies per channel, not the half-amplitude BNC-T split of n_TOF.
  • One copy goes to the digitizers: a V1742 for the 32 wall channels (borrow it if LNL's pool can: it retires when the walls go per bar end), a V1730S for the 8 PMTs.
  • The other copy goes into the n_TOF N1081B logic: walls, plastics, legs, PAIR/SINGLE.
  • New in the logic: the prescaler (the N1081B SCALER divides), a veto on DREAM's busy, and a trigger word latched by the V1730.

Trigger + scintillator DAQ: about €84k (61–115k).

Do we need an FPGA?

  • Not for B. The N1081Bs are FPGA boards with ready-made functions, and the V1730's own FPGA does the self-triggering.
  • C needs a programmable FPGA unit, the DT5495 (€5–12k with mezzanines and software), with firmware we write ourselves. In return the V1730s make the trigger signals themselves: no splits, no fan-outs, about €26k less than B.
  • C′ needs no FPGA: one V1730S per arm makes the arm's leg on board, and one N1081B combines the four arms.

Why not just n_TOF again?

  • The CERN rentals don't come with us.
  • The passive split cost us amplitude.
  • Wedged N1081Bs waited days for someone at the crate: switched PDUs fix that.
  • Matching DREAM to the scintillator data needed the n_TOF bunch structure. At a DC beam, the busy veto keeps DREAM's events and the digitizer's events one to one.

The SiPM walls: ganged at LNL, per bar end later (updated 30 Oct)

  • What the walls are (INFN documentation): each bar end is read by 2 channels of a 16-pixel linear SiPM array that spans 2 bars. That is 80 channels per wall, 320 in all, and every channel already leaves the wall on its own micro-coax.
  • At n_TOF, external boards summed the micro-coax into the ganged LEMO outputs (4 bars per channel, 8 per wall). Bias is ~54 V at ~8 µA per array, not the 63 V / 10 A once assumed.
  • No single readout does both jobs. The ganged LEMOs are amplified analog pulses, which want a digitizer. The raw micro-coax wants a SiPM front end (FERS).
  • FERS is what scales. Boards of 64 channels on one concentrator, so 160 → 320 is just adding boards. The PMT digitizer is needed in every path and carries over.
  • Plan for LNL: the ganged walls on a borrowed digitizer plus a bought PMT digitizer, about €32k with nothing stranded.
  • In parallel: a custom micro-coax → FERS board with INFN, and bench tests on a borrowed FERS (architecture D below). FERS at LNL itself costs about the same, ~€44k with the PMTs, if its trigger out passes the bench test in time.
  • Physics: no LNL reach gain from per-bar readout once the plastics' PMT time makes the time-of-flight cut. Per bar end gives a cleaner trigger, all 20 bars and a 3× tighter road on the Micromegas. Reading the 2 channels of a bar end separately (320) buys nothing.

Details: daq_trigger/SIPM_READOUT.md (§7 staging, out/sipm_staged.csv) and sipm_wall/README.md (Geant4 on the LNL samples).

        DREAM's busy, the event lock and the live time

        How the lock works

        • DREAM is non-paralysable: about 298 µs per event (RAW, 20 samples), set by each FEU's 83 MB/s link.
        • The final OR in the trigger (M4) is vetoed by BUSY = DREAM busy OR digitizer busy OR hold. The hold is a 1–10 µs N1081B gate that covers the moment before DREAM's busy rises.
        • So DREAM never refuses a trigger: event k in DREAM is event k in the digitizer, and the scalers measure the live time directly.
        • If the TCM busy line can't be used, a fixed 330–350 µs N93B gate does the same job, for a small extra dead time.

        Coincidence window and randoms

        A trigger built from digitizer or front-end discriminators (C, C′, D) moves in clock steps: the V1730 coincidence window comes in 8 ns steps, and its trigger-request pulses are 16 ns wide by default. The n_TOF analog chain ran a 20 ns leg window. At LNL a wider window costs nothing. At the high-rate DC sources (Birmingham, FRM II) the two-arm randoms grow linearly with the window and eat DREAM's live time.

        Load and amperage

        • Only the N1081Bs: a 6-board NIM bin draws about 200 W (1 A), so any switched PDU works. A NETIO 4KS (≈€290) on that bin is the cheapest option. Each wedge then costs a whole-bin cycle.
        • Per board: the DT1081B is the desktop version of the N1081B (90 mA at 220 V). If CAEN prices it like the N1081B, each board gets its own outlet, and 12 NIM slots (one bin, ≈€4k) are freed.
        • Whole DAQ: about 2.1 kW (9 A at 230 V). Use two PDUs on two 16 A circuits for inrush margin.
        • Free option: the boards' SSH port (22) stayed up through every wedge. If CAEN grants access, a soft reboot costs nothing and the PDU becomes the second line of defence.

        Actions

        1. Solder a LEMO onto the DREAM TCM busy output (Dylan). Then measure its level, its polarity, its rise after a trigger and its fall after readout, and whether it covers the FEU watermark throttle.
        2. Ask CAEN for quotes: N1081B vs DT1081B (×6), N979 with ×2 gain (×3), N625, V1742, V1730S, a VME crate with an optical link, DT5495 with A395D, used N93B, FERS A5202 or A5204 (×4) + DT5215.
        3. Ask CAEN: whether SCALER ÷P works for odd P, SSH reboot access, the latency of the V1730 LVDS trigger-request outputs, the FERS pair AND on arbitrary channels and its T-OUT latency/jitter, and whether a FERS can take the ganged LEMO pulses through an attenuator.
        4. Ask INFN (wall builders): were planes 2–4 rebuilt like plane 1; the SiPM model; the summing boards' schematic and micro-coax connector (for the custom board); the FERS they used on the bench (model, loan); their Sr-90 results.
        5. Ask CEA: the TCM's maximum latency, an external clock or timestamp-reset input, and whether it latches an input pattern.
        6. Ask LNL: whether their pool has V1730/V1742 digitizers (one to borrow for the ganged walls), NIM bins, FIFOs or an N93B; how many 16 A circuits; a UPS.
