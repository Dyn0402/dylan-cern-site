What we learned from how you and others make lithium targets

Lithium metal is always made off the beam line, then carried to it under argon or under vacuum. Nothing needs to be deposited in the beam pipe

Your layout is the robust one. A film on a metal stop that carries the heat lasts. The thin-film route broke at LNL: on AN2000 most LiF films gave 6–8 h of stable rate, the best two days. The one that lasted elsewhere, at MEG II, sat on 25 µm of copper.

20 µm of rolled lithium pressed on copper is already your routine. It is exactly our film and our stop, made without any evaporation.

We do not need anything deposited in the beam pipe. If our question of last week read that way, it was not what we meant: we had a coating chamber in mind. We found no laboratory that deposits Li, Cu or Ti on the line.

TARGET_REVIEW.md (11 Nov 2026) §2 and §3, sources in its §10 (literature search of 11 Nov; quotes should be checked in the source before they go into a talk). No laboratory was found that deposits Li, Cu or Ti inside the beam pipe. Li metal is always made off the line (glovebox, or an evaporation stand with a vacuum transfer). A 20 µm film does not need more: a few nm to ~0.5 µm of surface compound from the transfer is ≤ 3 % of it. The closest thing to in-place deposition is Novosibirsk’s separate evaporation stand, whose target leaves under vacuum behind its own gate valve. Compound films (LiF, Li₂O, LiPON) are what the pair experiments chose for chemical stability. They pay ×4–8 in yield.

The lithium: 10–12 µm instead of 20

Residual water turns Li into LiOH. The lithium atoms stay, but the O and H add stopping, so the beam slows down to the strong 441 keV resonance inside the film. This holds for every target, the cup and the ogive included

Li 20 µm

Li 15 µm

Li 12 µm

Li 10 µm

Half of a 20 µm Li film turned to LiOH by residual water takes a 1.10 MeV beam down to 454 keV: 52 % of the γ₀ then comes from the 441 keV resonance (fresh 0.85 %; 91 % at 1.03 MeV).

10–12 µm of Li stays at or below 10 % even fully converted, for 51–61 % of the yield. You already roll foils below 10 µm.

TARGET_REVIEW.md (11 Nov 2026) §4.2. review_li_conversion.py → out/target/options/review_li_conversion.csv: our yield model with PSTAR stopping, the outer fraction of the Li atoms converted to LiOH and the rest metal, at 1.10 MeV. Hover a point for the energy at which the beam leaves the film. The 441 keV resonance (17.64 MeV) is about 50× stronger than the 18.15 MeV one, so once the beam reaches it the 17.64 MeV pairs become most of what the detector sees. It did not matter for the neutron work, where the film’s energy loss only shapes the neutron spectrum. The cap column is an estimate until run L14 is in: the 45° diamond window’s Geant4 days with the thinner film. What we would do: 10–12 µm, run at 1.10 MeV rather than 1.03, watch the γ₀ yield per µC (it rises as the beam reaches 441 keV), and measure the rate with one film on the line for a week before the run.

Details

TARGET_REVIEW.md (11 Nov 2026) §6.3, §4.1, §4.7. Electroforming: CERN made a 200 µm-wall Cu chamber this way. Pressing a flat foil into a tip of R 2.8 mm stretches it, and lithium that overlaps the 65° flank is ×2.4 thicker along the beam. A disc that stays on the tip cap (r ≤ 2.5 mm) takes 55 % of a σ 2 mm beam. A rounder tip (R 6–8 mm) would take 95 %, which is a question for Pierfrancesco and needs a Geant4 check. σ68 of the opening angle with the big plastics: Cu ogive 18.5°, Al 0.4 mm 11.5°. The full study of the 4 Nov design, kept as the record: notes/lnl-cu-liner. The interactive 3D views: facilities/lnl-target.html.

Your view?

1 · How do you intend to put the lithium on your ogive: pressed foil with new moulds for the pointed tip, or evaporation? Your 2015 paper mentions a lithium evaporator. Does it work today, and how does a target leave it?

2 · Given how your group works, would you rather have a part on which your rolled and pressed lithium works directly?

3 · Would you consider a flat face at 45° instead of the point? It keeps your method and removes the flank.

4 · Can the LNL workshop turn a 0.4–0.5 mm aluminium wall? Can the targets group put 10–15 µm of copper on a flat aluminium face below ~150 °C, or would a plating shop do it?

5 · The vacuum at the target on the line you have in mind: pressure, residual gas, PVC or ceramic insulator. Could a film sit there for a week under beam while we watch the 478 keV and γ₀ lines?

6 · How thin can a rolled foil be made reproducibly (we would like 8–12 µm), and how is it carried from the glovebox to the line?

7 · The beam spot at the target, σ and halo, as before.
