Does det4's charge-spreading kernel survive a 25.64° rotation?

The flat-mount kernel, re-measured on the same detector tilted to 25.64° in the same run — plus the drift ladder that rotation buys, used to bound how much of the kernel is transverse diffusion.

run_63, Ar/CF4/iso 88/10/2, resist 769.8 V, ZS 4σ, 60 ns sampling · uRWELL telescope as the external reference · generated by measure.py + make_report.py

The kernel holds. The X view is at normal incidence in every arm, flat and rotated alike, so it isolates the kernel from track geometry. Across a 25.64° mount rotation and a 3.1× drift-field range its ±1 amplitude, area and delay stay within 5–12 % of the trend the rotated arms define, and the ±1 delay is 48 ± 3 ns in all four.

The geometry signature is unmistakable, and it is not the kernel. The Y view — normal in the flat mount, carrying the ladder in the rotated one — goes 3 → 8 strips of extent, and its ±1 delay flips from symmetric (-8 ns, the RC signature) to strongly antisymmetric (-104 ns, the ladder) — RC sharing is symmetric in ±d by construction, a drift ladder is not. ±2/±1 grows 2.8× at matched ±2 detection (0.218 → 0.618). The larger factor the stacks give is partly the 4 σ threshold, not the track — see the correction below.

Transverse diffusion is not what the kernel is made of. The lateral rms about the telescope impact point is field-independent (0.759 mm at 233 V/cm, 0.752 mm at 108 V/cm) where diffusion would have grown as E−1/2 to 1.11 mm. That bounds the diffusion term at < 0.16 mm rms, under 5 % of σ².

Why this run is an A/B and not a comparison

run_63 contains both mounts, 90 minutes apart, either side of one H4 TAX access (FLAT_CF4_RUN63.md). Same run number, same gas, resist held at 769.8 V throughout, ZS at 4σ in both. Only the mount angle and the drift field differ — and the drift field is itself the second lever.

Which view is tilted, and how we know

Not from the alignment matrix: det4 is rolled +90.2°, so the strips det4_sps_map calls “x” measure the detector's Y coordinate and the row norms of A mislead. Measured instead from the data, by fitting peak time against strip position event by event and taking the signed median slope — a ladder has a coherent sign, normal incidence scatters about zero:

M3 — is any of it transverse diffusion?

σT ∝ E−1/2, so dropping the field from 233 to 108 V/cm must inflate diffusion by 1.47×. The film does not care. Measuring the lateral rms about the telescope-predicted impact point (not det4's own centroid, which would be circular) therefore separates them:

The v-from-ladder column is a truncation diagnostic, and it is why only rot d425 carries the headline. Drift velocity must fall with field; instead the implied v rises (10.0 → 11.3 → 33.3 µm/ns) because the ladder overruns the 3.84 µs window at low field and the fitted slope flattens. d325 is partly truncated and d225 is badly truncated.

Correction — how much of the ±2 jump is the threshold?

Asked directly, and it matters: both arms are ZS at 4σ, but their ±2 detection fractions are not the same (0.87 flat vs 0.98 angled at the loose gate), because at an angle the ±2 strip genuinely carries more charge and clears threshold more often. So the censoring is matched for ±1 but not for ±2, and part of the raw contrast is the threshold rather than the geometry.

Re-gating the flat arm to a higher central amplitude brings its ±2 detection to 0.996, matching the angled arm's 0.976. At that matched censoring the geometry factor is 2.8×, not the 4.8× the trimmed-mean stacks suggest. Two independent checks that 2.8× is the right answer: the flat number at matched detection (0.218) agrees with the RAW flat measurement of 0.249 (TWOGAS_HEADON) to 12 %, which is the ZS–RAW offset that document predicts; and the loose-gate flat number is inflated by taking a maximum over a noisy weak trace.

Correction — the cosmic bench is RAW, not zero-suppressed

An earlier reading of the det3 bench drift scan blamed zero suppression. That was wrong: the bench calibration windows carry 37 % of strips peaking below 20 ADC against a ~7 ADC noise σ (a 5σ cut would sit near 37), samples reaching −411, and a populated per-strip noise field. Nothing is suppressed. The real cause is window truncation, and it is measurable directly — the central strip's last sample, as a fraction of its peak:

The window is 32 × 60 ns = 1.92 µs; below ~700 V the column takes longer than that and the delayed, dispersed shared copy is exactly the part that falls off the end. Restricting to the untruncated points (700–1100 V) and holding the central amplitude fixed at 1000–1600 ADC, the Y-plane ±1 ratio moves 0.564 → 0.591, +5 % over a 1.57× field range — consistent with the SPS RAW invariance, not with diffusion. The X plane still walks +33 % over the same range and is not explained by either truncation or selection; X's sharing is the weak, ambiguous channel (±2/±1 ≈ 0.11) and this stays open.

Can we measure the waveform kernel itself?

SUPERSEDED 2026-08-18 — this section's numbers are wrong, its conclusion is not. Two defects, both fixed in The sharing kernel, measured:

  1. Stale input. The deconvolution below consumed the pre-made d4_kernel_fit_raw*.npz, which are dated 08-03 and predate the clean selection. Their peak-aligned central stack sits at 12.4 % of peak before the pulse exists, against 1.7 % for the clean stacks — so the 17–18 % "negative weight at t < 0" quoted below is that artefact, not the kernel. Only two of the three RAW drift plateaus were used, and the missing one (700 V) is the best populated.
  2. The method was harder than it needed to be. Deconvolution needs a regulariser, and λ moved the answer by ±10 % in area and 250 → 400 ns in tail τ. The cross-relation n0 ∗ Wd = nd ∗ W0 tests the same physics with nothing inverted — both sides are measured data convolved with a model filter.

What stands: the cancellation argument in the next paragraph, that the shipped kernel form cannot make the measured tail, and that this only works head-on. What does not: every number in the table below, and in particular the tail τ — it turns out to depend on how much tail the fit window contains, so it is not a constant at all.

Yes — and only from head-on data. At normal incidence every strip sees the same ionisation column, so each strip's waveform is one common signal C(t) (column arrival folded with the amplifier) through a lateral transfer function:

Wd(t) = (hd * C)(t)  ⇒  Wd(f)/W0(f) = Gd(f)

The column and the electronics cancel exactly in the ratio. So gd(t) is the sharing kernel itself — no shaper template, no drift model, no vdrift, no assumed functional form. The cancellation holds only at normal incidence, which is precisely why the flat runs are the ones that can do this and the 25.64° ones cannot.

The measured kernel is a prompt spike plus a one-sided ~280 ns exponential tail. The model assumes a symmetric 12 ns Gaussian translated by 146 ns. These are different objects. The measured ±2 kernel is later still (centroid +733 ns, 2.4× the ±1's) and nearly a plateau out past 1.7 µs — the cascade signature. The kernel is also stable between the two fields (areas within 8 %, ±2 centroid identical to 1 ns), so it is a layer property, as the amplitude work already said.

This is how you get the kernels: deconvolve them from RAW head-on data, and use the measured gd(t) in place of the (c1, c2, τs, σs) parameterisation. Note the impulse response itself is already measured — wft.calibrate.measure_templates takes it from bright inclined bench strips. It is only the sharing kernel that is currently assumed.

Systematics on the deconvolution. The area is stable to about ±10 % over a 20× range of the Wiener regularisation (λ = 0.005–0.10 gives 0.574–0.458); the tail τ is more λ-sensitive (288–434 ns) and should be quoted as 250–400 ns, not to three figures. Per-sample acceptance falls to ~0.37 at the window edges even in RAW, so the far tail is the least certain part. The X view does not deconvolve cleanly — its +d and −d kernels disagree by 30 % and 18 % of the weight lands at t < 0 — consistent with the standing X tilt, so only Y is quoted.

What this does not establish

  • Mount and field are confounded. There is exactly one flat arm and it is the only one at 233 V/cm, so “does rotation change the kernel” is answered by extrapolating the rotated arms' field trend by 91 V/cm. A flat arm at 142 V/cm would settle it outright; none exists in run_63.
  • Zero-suppressed throughout. Every arm is ZS 4σ, so absolute values are not comparable to the RAW arms — TWOGAS_HEADON F3 sized the ZS–RAW offset at ~6 % on the ±1 peak and ~18 % on the area. Only the flat-vs-rotated difference is meant to be read here.
  • The diffusion bound is weaker than it looks. Censoring worsens at low field (strips per sample 1.91 → 1.62), biasing the width down exactly where diffusion should push it up — the same sign as the null. The better-controlled statement is the ±1 peak ratio at matched detection fraction, which moves the wrong way for diffusion.
  • d225 is 307 events and its ladder is badly truncated; it is shown for completeness, not weight.
  • The deconvolution is one detector, two fields, Y view only, and it inherits the run_71 mean-waveform selection (strong central strip, oscillating channels 510/372 masked). It is a first measurement, not a calibration product — turning it into one means a per-plane, per-detector gd(t) with its own error band, and a wft kernel form that can accept a tabulated kernel instead of four numbers.
  • One detector, one gas, one tilt angle. This is det4 in Ar/CF4/iso at 25.64°. Nothing here licenses transporting the numbers to another chamber — the standing rule that a kernel is per-detector is unchanged.
