1

Details

"Trusted" here: a pulse of about one minimum-ionising particle is found and reconstructed within 10 % of its true amplitude in at least 90 % of cases, pile-up with real beam-induced pulses included, and the photodetector gain (measured from the data) is back within 5 %. The walls and liquids have no gain sag that the data can see or separate; for them only the reconstruction counts.

Three independent pieces: (1) pulses injected into the real recorded flash tails and reconstructed (reconstruction bias and resolution); (2) the detector gain from the shape of the measured amplitude spectra (the injection cannot see gain); (3) Geant4 for what particles arrive when. Energy from the cosmic muons at 30–32 ms and the muon calibration. The first look (baselines, pulse shape) is the earlier note: scint-flash-recovery.

Details

Amplitudes: walls 8–120 mV per bar end (0.3–4 MIP), plastics 25–800 mV (0.45–14 MeVee), liquids 25–400 mV. Each pass adds a comb of pulses with a random offset; 12 passes per amplitude, per channel, per bunch. A pulse is found if a peak lies within 40 ns (walls) / 6 ns (plastics, liquids) of it.

"Clean" pulses: no real pulse at least 5 % of their size within a pulse width (template above 5 % of peak: 217 / 42 / 18 ns). They isolate the flash tail itself; all pulses include the beam-induced pile-up, which is what an analysis gets.

3

Details

At 19.5 m a neutron arriving 1 µs after the flash has ~2 MeV; at 2 µs ~0.5 MeV. Its proton recoils then give less than 0.1 MeVee (quenching), so from ~2 µs everything above threshold is e± from γ: capture γ in the apparatus (hydrogen 2.2 MeV dominates in the simulation). In the simulation the deposit spectrum keeps one shape from ~2 µs to ~1 ms (95 % quantile 1.99 MeVee, the 2.2 MeV Compton edge), then hardens slightly.

Rates: the simulation is within ×0.6 of the data on the plastics at 3–300 µs (Geant4 / data = 1.77) but ×5 low after 1 ms, and its spectrum is softer than the data (data 95 % quantile behind a wall hit 4.6–5.3 MeVee, simulation ~2 MeVee). Not simulated: the experimental hall (room-return neutrons, captures in concrete and the dump), the in-beam γ of the spallation target, the flash itself. The beam profile is the pre-2026-10-14 one (5× too narrow). So Geant4 explains what arrives early, not all of what arrives late.

Code: g4_mix.py (condor over full_sim/neutrons_fullrange), g4_mix_analysis.py.

Details

The pile-up probability is the beam-induced occupancy: the pulse rate times the pulse width. It falls with the rate, roughly in proportion to the beam intensity (dashed = parasitic, half the protons). It is not a property of the flash and does not go away with better electronics; a narrower pulse or a multi-pulse fit (used here) reduces what it costs.

Clean pulses are rare where the occupancy is high, so their points start late on the walls and plastics; points with fewer than 100 clean pulses are not drawn.

Details

If a pulse is spoiled only by a neighbour arriving at random, the fraction left intact is exp(−a R) with R the pulse rate per channel and a an effective width (µs). a is fitted on the injection bins after the tail itself has stopped mattering (walls ≥ 10 µs, plastics and liquids ≥ 5 µs), with R the measured rate of pulses above 4 mV in the same channels and bunches; fit residuals 0.02–0.05 for ~1 MIP. The rate is measured to 10 ms, so the model reaches times the injection cannot. Fitted a (~1 MIP): liquid ded 0.008 µs; liquid par 0.005 µs; plastic ded 0.025 µs; plastic par 0.014 µs; SiPM wall (per end) ded 0.502 µs; SiPM wall (per end) par 0.299 µs.

Details

The earlier note read the 95 % edge of the plastic spectrum behind a wall hit as an upper bound on the gain sag (0.71 / 0.87 / 0.93 at 10 / 100 / 300 µs). The quantiles separate the two: from ~20 µs the median and the 99 % point are back within 2–4 % while the 75–95 % points are still 10–20 % low; a gain sag cannot do that, a change in the share of 2–5 MeVee pulses can. Before ~10 µs all five drop by the same factor (0.37 at 3 µs, ~0.6 at 5 µs, ~0.8 at 8 µs), which is what a gain sag looks like. A PMT whose base is drained by the ~0.5 V of pile-up current in the first µs behaves this way.

Walls: the top of the spectrum is within 1.07–1.00 from 3 µs on (a few % above 1 early: piled pulses are bigger), and dips 3 % at 0.2–1 ms. No gain sag the data can resolve beyond that. Liquids: their quantiles move by ×1.4–2.9 at 2–100 µs, so the shape changes far too much to read a gain from them.

Caveat: this assumes the top of the spectrum is made by the same particles at all times; the simulation says the population is capture γ from ~2 µs, but it does not reproduce the hardest part of the data spectrum.

Code: gain_ref.py.

Details

Reconstruction bias: median A_fit/A of the injected pulses over the same before the flash (all pulses, pile-up included, multi-pulse fit). Gain: q99 of the spectrum (previous slide), plastics behind a wall hit and wall bars; the liquids have none. The total is the bias of a reconstructed energy if nothing is corrected. Both parts are smooth in time and measured, so a correction g(t) can be applied; what remains is its uncertainty, ~the spread between the plastic populations (coincident vs singles, ~0.1 at 5–8 µs, < 0.03 after 15 µs).

What this does not rule out

· The gain estimate assumes the top of the spectrum is made by the same particles at every time. Geant4 supports a stable capture-γ population from ~2 µs, but does not reproduce the hardest part of the data spectrum.

· Before ~2 µs (fast-neutron recoils) gain and mix cannot be separated on the plastics and liquids.

· Injection works only inside the unsuppressed head block (walls 40–60 µs, plastics ~250 µs, liquids 100–200 µs). Later times (the walls' headline) come from the pile-up model: exp(−a × measured rate), fitted where both exist.

· Pulses are injected with the family template; per-channel shape differences cancel against the pre-flash control.

· Pile-up scales with the pulse rate: these numbers hold for this apparatus at 4 and 8.5×10¹² protons per pulse.
