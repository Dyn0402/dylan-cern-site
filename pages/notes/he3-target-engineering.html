Details

σ68² = 10.86² + (1.41·θ₀)², θ₀ the Highland angle at 10 MeV/c through the radial material, reproduces the balloon (10.8°), the cells (11.3–11.4°) and the capsule (14.4°). The ~11° floor is the Micromegas segment direction, not the target. Days = K·σ68/L_beam with K from the 30 bar cell. Not in it: the beam-induced background scaling with the beam area (about 10 % of the 30 bar cell's background), so small-beam designs are probably a little better than drawn and need Geant4. he3_target.py → out/designs.csv, out/calibration.csv.

4

Details

Sizing rules (he3_target.py): filament-wound CFRP by netting analysis, t = 1.5·P·R/σ with σ = 500 MPa (1500 MPa lamina strength, burst factor 3), ≥ 0.3 mm, on a non-structural metal liner; monolithic Be or Al at min(UTS/4, YS/1.5): Be 86 MPa (Materion I-70-H minimums), Al 2219-T851 114 MPa. The rule reproduces the n_TOF capsule as built (500 bar at R 10 mm needs 1.5 mm; it has 0.6 Al + 0.9 CFRP). Be tubes are gun-drilled and turned from block; thin-wall practice is ~0.4 mm at small diameter (ask Materion for Ø80–100 × 400 mm).

Every candidate is either below the pressure-equipment threshold or in Category I

UK Pressure Equipment (Safety) Regulations 2016 = PED Annex II Table 2 (vessels, group 2 gases); PS = 1.1 × fill

Under 1 L, even 500 bar needs no category: the capsule, the 200 bar tube.

The 30 bar cells are Category I (module A).

In service the whole system falls under PSSR 2000: a written scheme of examination.

5

Details

Thresholds: SI 2016/1105 reg. 6 (as the PED Art 4): group 2 gas vessels are in scope above V = 1 L with PS·V > 50 bar·L, or PS > 1000 bar. Category I up to 200 bar·L; the higher boundaries come from secondary sources and need checking. PSSR 2000 applies to any system above 0.5 bar gauge with a relevant fluid: the cell, the gas panel and the storage cylinder need a written scheme of examination by a competent person, whatever the class. Birmingham's own pressure-systems procedure applies on top (ask). A custom vessel is not an ADR/TPED receptacle: it travels empty and is filled on site (ask a dangerous-goods adviser).

Gas in and out: a closed loop where nothing vents and the cryopump does both jobs

6

Details
  1. Fill. Evacuate and bake the cell and lines, leak-check the panel. Expand the cylinder into the cryopump at 4–10 K (charcoal adsorbs helium). Close it off, open it to the cell, warm the charcoal: the gas compresses into the cell with no moving parts in the ³He. Top up in steps; meter the inventory on P2 before every warm-up.
  2. Recover. Cool the cryopump, open it to the cell: charcoal at 4 K pumps to < 10⁻³ mbar. Warm into the cylinder (through a compressor if the cylinder sits above what thermal compression reaches). 1 mbar left in 2.3 L is 2×10⁻³ L STP.
  3. Losses per campaign: residual < 0.01 L, leaks at 10⁻⁶ mbar·L/s for 60 days 0.005 L, a relief event 0, regeneration < 0.1 L: ≲ 0.2 % of 62 L.
  4. 200 bar and above: a small all-metal diaphragm compressor (³He/tritium compatibility is a vendor question) is the extra cost of the t200 option.
  5. Precedent and help: the ILL ³He group (Tyrex fills and recovers cells daily), ISIS, PSI, the detector makers (tubes at 10–40 atm). The JLab tritium/³He target (40 cm Al 2219 cells, 20 bar ³He, welded, valves outside the acceptance, evacuated chamber as secondary containment, safety factor ≥ 5; arXiv:1306.6000) is the closest nuclear-physics precedent. Borrowing a working panel, or its people, is quicker than building one.

7

Details

Price: ~2–2.5 k$/L for bulk gas is the most-cited figure (DOE Isotope Program 2024–25, secondary sources); purified grades are quoted to ~10 k$/L, and there is no transparent spot price. Demand is rising (dilution refrigerators); the DOE has signed forward purchases of lunar ³He for 2029. Treat 2.5 k€/L as optimistic. Quantities: baseline 62 L, fallback 21 L, 200 bar tube 90 L, plus ~5 % for lines and the cylinder heel.

The beam turns some ³He into tritium: about 1 MBq per run, far below the exemption

³He(n,p)t: σ = 1.02 b against σ_nγ = 53 µb over the Birmingham spectrum (1.9×10⁴ tritons per capture)

room-return thermal, per 10⁴ n/cm²/s (not simulated)

~1.5×10⁵ Bq per g of gas: under the 10⁶ Bq/g exemption, over 100 Bq/g, so it is radioactive material under an exemption. The RPA has to agree it.

The getter takes the tritium out before the gas goes back.

Details

Asks to send now: Birmingham (pressure-systems procedure, room for a gas panel and dump tank, RPA on tritiated ³He, LHe/LN₂ on site); n_TOF (the 500 bar capsule's maker, code, liner, laminate, who filled it and to what pressure); ISIS / ILL ³He group / PSI (a 60–100 L loan for ~3 months, a panel or a fill service, return conditions); a COPV shop (Al-lined CFRP Ø80 × 400 mm at 30 bar, Ø40 at 200 bar); Materion only if Be stays in play; ³He suppliers (price, lead time, buy-back, licensing).

what this does not settle

Open: whether ³He can be had in time, what Birmingham's rules add, and whether the smaller beam of a high-pressure tube helps the room loads.

Not settled here

Days for unsimulated designs are interpolations (±15 %), without the beam-area dependence of the background.

PED boundaries above 200 bar·L, ADR transport and the ³He price come from secondary sources.

The room and the shield wall remain the larger risk for the measurement (TARGET_GEANT).

Baseline 30 bar R 4 (62 L) or the 200 bar R 2 tube (90 L): after Geant4 item 2.
