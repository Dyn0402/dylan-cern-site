Details

Question. On the cosmic bench every MX17 chamber shows its mesh centre 1.2–2.3 mm below a point 150 mm out, with the cathode roughly flat (nTof_x17 rerun/model_audit/followups/fb_bow_maps). The chamber runs a few mbar over atmosphere, which visibly bulges the 60 µm window. Does the same pressure bow the readout PCB by the measured amount, and how much does the Rohacell taped behind the board help?

Answer. No. A large-deflection two-layer plate model of the board over the 402 mm aperture gives, with the mounting's own edge stiffness, a bench-equivalent dish of 0.25–0.44 mm at 3 mbar and 0.38–0.59 mm at 5 mbar (bonded – loose Rohacell, 1.6 mm PCB). No combination of thickness (1.2–2.4 mm), bond, edge or mesh tension reaches 1.2 mm below ~15 mbar. The measured profile shape is also not a pressure shape. The rest of the dish needs another origin (board warp, mounting) or the bench depth is over-read.

What would settle it: measure the window sag (it is a pressure gauge, slide 6), change the overpressure and re-measure the tilt route, and put a straightedge on an unpressurised board.

Details

Model (MX17_Geant/mechanics/pcb_bow/edge1d.py): the board in cylindrical bending, 0.25 mm cells, from the centre to the overhang end at 235 mm. Supports: Rohacell foundation outside 201 mm (k = E/t = 1.4×10¹⁰ N/m³, compression only, so the board may lift); the frame face as a rigid one-sided contact over 205–206.55 and 210.25–220 mm ("bottomed"); the O-ring's sealing reaction (2000 N/m assumed) pushing down at 208.4 mm; gas inside the seal. Alternative: the frame rides on the O-ring (no metal contact), which is then a preloaded line spring (10⁶–10⁷ N/m²).

Result. K_eff = M/θ at the aperture edge is 700 / 1110 / 1830 N·m/m for a 1.3 / 1.6 / 2.0 mm board with the frame bottomed (K·a/D ≈ 50–90), almost independent of pressure, the sealing force (0 or 2000 N/m), a 20 µm foam precompression (+40 %) or a softer foam grade (−15 %). The board lifts by < 40 µm anywhere outside the aperture. With the frame riding on the O-ring the edge softens with pressure (K ≈ 460 N·m/m at 10 mbar) and the rim pivots about the seal, lifting ~0.5 mm at the overhang.

In 2-D the K_eff values are used as the spring edge of the plate model ("real edge" from here on). Earlier estimate K = E b³/12t ≈ 3900 N·m/m (15 mm foam pinch) was ~3.5× too stiff; the dish changes by +5–7 %. Ideal clamped vs simply supported, for scale: 0.55 vs 0.76 mm at 5 mbar (1.6 mm, loose Rohacell).

Details

Why. Rohacell helps by turning the board into a sandwich: the PCB is one skin, the 25 µm mylar on the far face of the foam the other, and the foam has to carry the shear between them. That shear flow is largest near the supports and zero at the centre (as in any uniformly loaded beam), so a bond near the edge does most of the work: a 60 mm rim band captures 78 %, a centre that has come unstuck out to r = 100 mm costs nothing (98 %), while a central patch alone captures only 45 %.

Size of the effect. Loose → glued: 0.57 → 0.38 mm at 5 mbar (−33 %). Without the mylar skin (foam glued, nothing on its far face) the gain is about half of that. Tape that only joins the foam's edge to the board outside the aperture is the "loose" case. A bad bond therefore makes the bow at most ~1.5× the well-bonded value.

Practical: if re-taping, cover the outer 60 mm all round with a continuous tape (or full-face transfer adhesive), and make sure the back mylar is bonded to the foam: without that second skin the sandwich has nothing to work with. Patterns were run with the earlier foam-pinch edge (K = 3900 N·m/m); the fractions are insensitive to the edge.

Interface model: shear spring k(x, y) = 10¹² N/m³ (glued) inside the green area, 0 elsewhere; full-face tapes k = G/t: 10⁸ (soft foam tape, ~0.1 MPa over 1 mm), 10⁹ (acrylic PSA 0.1 MPa / 100 µm), 10¹⁰.

How strongly it sticks hardly matters: a soft tape is already 85 % of glue

Details

Mechanism. The mesh is laminated on the board (bulk pillars). Its tension T is balanced inside the board by an equal compression, so the pair carries no net in-plane force: no stress stiffening or softening of the bend. What is left is the moment of the mesh about the board's neutral surface, m = T·e with e ≈ 0.8 mm (PCB neutral surface 0.66 mm below the top + 0.15 mm gap). It cups the board toward the mesh side (centre away from the cathode, the measured sign). A uniform moment does nothing to a perfectly clamped plate; with the real edge stiffness a little of it gets through.

Numbers. T = 10 N/m gives m ≈ 0.008 N·m/m: +0.3 µm. T = 10 N/cm (1000 N/m, the range quoted for stretched meshes) gives +25 µm on the real edge and +45 µm on a simply supported one, close to additive with the pressure (the two couple only through the small membrane stiffening).

Floating-mesh case (mesh held by the frame and resting on pillars, not laminated): its tension then acts on the mesh alone. A dish of curvature κ needs a pressure T·κ to keep the mesh on the pillars, against the electrostatic pull ε₀E²/2 ≈ 48 Pa at 500 V over 150 µm. At 10 N/m, T·κ ≈ 0.6 Pa: the mesh follows the board. At 10 N/cm it would not follow beyond ~0.5 mm of dish. Not our case if the pillars are bulk, but it is the one way mesh tension could matter.

w0 = v·r_f·dh/du is the solid route of the bench study (fit errors ±0.02–0.1 µm/ns). For each view: weighted fit w0 = c + α·s(u), with s the model's mean slope in the same 9 bins (other coordinate averaged over the efficient area), taken at the pressure where that model's dish is 1.7 mm. Σχ² over 9 views (det7 X, with its faulty half, excluded), 63 dof: parabolic dish 1065, pressure, real edge 1431, pressure, simply supported 924, imposed edge rotation 864.

An edge-held plate's slope turns over before the rim (curvature reverses near a clamp); the data steepen toward it, most clearly on det2 Y, det3 X/Y, det4 X/Y and det6 Y. None of the shapes fits (best χ²/dof ≈ 14). Part of that is reconstruction near the strip ends (the skeptic review flagged own-axis edge effects). Read as geometry, curvature concentrated near the rim points to a load at the edge (mounting, frame) or a built-in board shape, not to a uniform pressure. The amplitude α, read through each shape with a flat cathode (r_f = ½), implies a dish of 2.8–3.0 mm.

summary · what this does not rule out · what to check

Pressure gives 0.4–0.6 mm at 5 mbar. The rest of the 1.2–2.3 mm needs another cause, or ~20–60 mbar of overpressure.

Not ruled out

• the bench overpressure is really ≥ 20 mbar (the window would bulge ≥ 16 mm)

• built-in board warp: normal fab tolerance 0.75 % ≈ 3 mm over 400 mm; it would survive vertical mounting at n_TOF

• a mounting load at the edge (frame flatness, screw torque, cables, M1 cards); the rim-steepening shape points there

• the bench depth over-read: ±0.4 mm reconstruction systematic and ≤ 0.6 mm M3 bow put the low end at 0.8–1.2 mm

Cheap checks

1. Window sag with a straightedge while flowing: ~8–10 mm → 3–5 mbar; ~20 mm → ~40 mbar

2. Double the overpressure, re-measure the w0 route: a pressure dish grows ×1.5, a warp does not move

3. Straightedge or dial gauge on an open, unpressurised board

4. The fab stack-up (t enters as t³) and how the Rohacell and its mylar are taped

Details

Model limits. Kirchhoff kinematics per layer (no core shear in the Rohacell; estimated < 1 % at this span); 41×41 grid (~1 %); the board stays in contact with the foam over the aperture (true under pressure); isotropic FR4 and an ideal square aperture; no initial warp, so the model gives the change from the unloaded shape; the window has no pre-tension.

Open questions for the hardware. Actual overpressure on the bench (bubbler height or outlet restriction), any observed window sag, the PCB stack-up from the fab, how the Rohacell is taped (full face, strips or edge) and whether the back mylar is bonded to it, and whether the frame bottoms on the board or rides on the O-ring.
